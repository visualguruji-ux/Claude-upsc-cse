## PART A — SCOPE OF THE SUBJECT & COMPARISON WITH OTHER SOCIAL SCIENCES

### A.0 Nature of Sociology (open every scope answer with this)
Sociology **is a scientific discipline** — it uses **objective, systematic methods** to study social
reality in the light of **empirical evidence** — **but it cannot be *directly* modelled on natural
science**, because human behaviour (conscious, meaning-giving, volitional) differs from the world of
objects. *(Robert Bierstedt, The Social Order:* sociology is **generalising** not special, a **social**
not natural science, and **pure** more than applied.*)*

### A.1 What is "scope"?
Scope = the **subject-matter / boundaries / field of inquiry** of a subject. Every science needs a
defined boundary to be studied systematically. **But there is no single agreed view on the scope of
sociology** — hence the classic **two-schools debate**.

### A.2 The Two Schools (classic academic debate — always cite)
**(i) Formalistic / Specialistic School** — *Georg Simmel* (leader), Vierkandt, Von Wiese, Tönnies,
Max Weber, Small.
- Sociology should be a **pure, independent, specific science with a *limited* scope**.
- It studies the **FORMS** of social relationships **abstracted from their CONTENTS**.
  - **Simmel:** separate *forms* (competition, cooperation, subordination, super-ordination, division
    of labour) from *contents* (economic, political, religious).
  - **Von Wiese:** forms = associative + dissociative processes. **Vierkandt:** ultimate psychic
    forms. **Weber:** interpretive understanding of *particular* forms of social behaviour.
- **Critique:** form & content are inseparable; pure abstraction is unreal; geometry/economics/
  international law also study forms; conception too narrow.

**(ii) Synthetic School** — *Émile Durkheim, L.T. Hobhouse, Morris Ginsberg, P.A. Sorokin.*
- Sociology is a **general, synthesising science** — a **synthesis of the social sciences**.
  - **Durkheim divided sociology into 3 parts:** ① **Social Morphology** (territorial substratum,
    population — size, density, distribution); ② **Social Physiology** (sociology of religion, morals,
    law, economic life); ③ **General Sociology** (general character of social facts, general laws).
  - **Ginsberg:** social morphology, social control, social processes, social pathology.
  - **Sorokin:** relationships & correlations between classes of social phenomena + their common
    features.
- **Critique:** synthesising *everything* risks losing a distinct identity.

### A.3 Adarsh's "Goal of Sociology" grid (value-addition framing)
Scope also depends on **what sociology aims to know** — three positions:

| Goal | Meaning | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| **OU of OR** | *Objective Understanding of Objective Reality* — scope restricted to hard facts | **Positivists** — Durkheim, Comte |
| **SU of OR** | *Subjective Understanding of Objective Reality* — **value-accommodation** | **Weber, Symbolic Interactionism** — *R.K. Mukherjee* argued values must be **accommodated** |
| **SU of SR** | *Subjective Understanding of Subjective Reality* | **Anti-positivists** — Schutz, Garfinkel |

- **Types of theory across the scope:** *Grand/universal theories* → *Ideal types* (Weber) →
  **Middle-Range Theories (Merton, MRT)** → concepts (e.g., Beck's *Risk Society*).
- Takeaway line: **"All sociology is contextual and, to a degree, ad-hoc"** — hence a *single* fixed
  scope is impossible.

### A.4 Historical evolution of the scope (Adarsh + LevelUp)
- **Initial Phase — Macro units:** Comte's **Social Statics & Dynamics**; **inductive/deductive**
  reasoning; Spencer's **organismic analogy** (society evolves like a body); **humanistic** strand
  (e.g., **Chicago School**).
- **2nd Phase — Formal vs Informal:** *Formal* → scope narrowed to **micro-phenomena** (Tönnies);
  *Informal* → "sociology can study everything" (Mannheim, Sorokin) → split into **General
  Sociology** & **Special/Historical Sociology**.
- **3rd Phase — Blending:** Parsons; **Merton's MRT & SOSA** synthesise structure + specificity.
- **Now — Multidisciplinary approach** (*Bryman*). *Howard Becker:* "Good sociology → meaningful
  descriptions of organisations, valid explanations of their origin, and realistic proposals for
  improvement."

### A.5 Scope by subject-matter (LevelUp handout — quick recall list)
Sociology studies: **Social institutions** (family, religion, education, economy, polity, media);
**Social control** — *formal* (law, fine, imprisonment) & *informal* (etiquette, **ridicule**,
**opprobrium/ostracism** — fatwa, excommunication); **Social networks** — *informal* (clique, kin) &
*formal* (bureaucracy, party); **Social pathology** (crime, casteism, sexism); **Social processes** —
*associative* (cooperation, assimilation) & *dissociative* (competition, conflict); **Social action**;
**Social change**.
- **Scope in India:** caste, village, joint family, religion; Sanskritic texts; agrarian classes;
  peasant/tribal/ethnic/women's movements. **Under globalisation:** westernisation's impact; social
  media & new movements (environmental, queer).

### A.6 Comparison with Other Social Sciences
**Relationship has evolved in 3 phases (handout):**
1. **Competitive beginnings (early 19thC):** sociology asserted a distinct domain — society is a
   *sui generis* reality, irreducible to economics/politics/psychology (**Durkheim**: study of
   **social facts**).
2. **Collaborative borrowings (mid 20thC, one-way):** sociology borrowed **fieldwork/participant
   observation** (Anthropology), **survey & sampling** (Statistics), **attitudes/socialisation**
   (Psychology), **rational choice/inequality** (Economics).
3. **Interdisciplinary convergence (late 20thC, two-way):** hybrid fields — **political sociology,
   environmental sociology, development studies**. *Multidisciplinary* = disciplines side-by-side
   (non-integrative); *Interdisciplinary* = integrated analysis.

**↔ HISTORY**
- **Radcliffe-Brown:** history & sociology "have no relation" — *history is conjectural/idiographic,
  sociology is empirical/nomothetic.* Sociology looks for **patterns**, history for **unique events**.
- **G.E. Howard:** "History is past sociology; sociology is present history." **Philip Abrams:**
  "sociology is deeply historical." **Sociology without history is rootless; history without
  sociology is fruitless.** **E.H. Carr:** the more sociological history is, the better.
- **Historical Sociology** studies **origin & development of institutions** (Ghurye on caste, **A.R.
  Desai** on nationalism, **Barrington Moore** *Social Origins of Dictatorship & Democracy*, Engels
  on family/property/state, Weber on rational capitalism), **comparative causes of change**, and
  **generalisations from history** (Bryan Wilson on secularisation; **Marx's historical materialism**;
  Durkheim on law & solidarity; Foucault's *History of Sexuality*).
- **Value-adds:** **Immanuel Wallerstein** — *World Systems Theory*; **Maurice Halbwachs** —
  *Collective Memory* (social groups shape historical narrative); **Theda Skocpol** (*States & Social
  Revolutions* — state breakdown + peasant revolt in France/Russia/China); **Subaltern history**
  (Ranajit Guha; Phule's *Ghulamgiri*; Ambedkar) vs **Colonial historiography**.

**↔ ECONOMICS**
- Economics studies **production–consumption–exchange** of goods/services in modern industrial
  society; assumes **Homo economicus** (rational utility-maximiser); **quantitative** (modelling,
  statistics). For sociology, the economic institution is **just one of many**; individuals are
  **social beings** whose economic acts are shaped by culture (e.g., wealth spent for **prestige** =
  Veblen's **conspicuous consumption**; charity; caste networks in job access).
- **Adam Smith:** "Eco > Socio"; **Durkheim:** "Socio > Eco." **Marx:** economic determinism (infra
  shapes supra). **Weber:** *Protestant Ethic (PESC)*.
- **Economic Sociology** value-adds: **Mark Granovetter** (embeddedness, network effects in labour
  markets); **Gary Becker** (Human Capital Theory); **Behavioural economics / Nudge**; **Barbara
  Wootton** (*Social Foundations of Wage Policy*); **gender budgeting, SECC, feminist economics,
  dependency theory, CSR, resource-mobilisation theory (Tilly)**; **Beteille** — intersection of
  caste & economic inequality.

**↔ PSYCHOLOGY**
- Psychology = the **individual's** perception, cognition, emotion, mental structure (**psychic
  facts**); prioritises **individual agency, brain, genetics** (Freud's **psychoanalysis**, trait
  theory). Sociology = **external social forces / social facts** (Radcliffe-Brown: Psychology =
  *mental systems*, Sociology = *social systems*). Psychology studies our troubles as **personal
  troubles**, sociology as **public issues** (e.g., suicide: *why does an individual* vs *why does the
  suicide-**rate** vary across groups*).
- **Bridges:** **Social psychology** (how groups shape individual attitudes); **Status & Role** —
  **Gerth & Mills:** the concept of **role bridges** sociology & psychology. **Cooley:** social self
  (the "Me"). **Mead:** social psychology.
- **Value-adds:** **C.W. Mills** — "isolated individuals and non-individual societies do not exist";
  **Oscar Lewis** — *subculture of poverty*; **Goffman** — dramaturgy; **Eric Hoffer** — maladjusted
  individuals initiate social movements; **Albert Cohen** — *status frustration*/subcultural theory
  of deviance; **Howard Becker** — labelling; **Judith Butler** — gender as performative "acts".

**↔ SOCIAL ANTHROPOLOGY**
- **A.L. Kroeber:** the two are **"twin sisters."** Anthropology historically studied **small, simple,
  pre-literate, "exotic"/tribal** societies **holistically** via **fieldwork/participant observation/
  ethnography** (Malinowski on Trobrianders/Arunta; Evans-Pritchard on the Nuer; Radcliffe-Brown on
  the Andamanese). Sociology studied **large, modern, complex, industrial** societies via **surveys &
  statistics**, focusing on *parts* (bureaucracy, modern law, education).
- **Fred Voget:** the difference is now more of **application** than of scope/concept/method.
- **Value-adds:** **James Frazer** — social anthropology = the branch of sociology dealing with
  primitive societies; **Radcliffe-Brown** — social anthropology is **"comparative sociology"**
  (nomothetic > idiographic); **G.S. Ghurye** — no student of Indian society can split anthropology &
  sociology; **André Béteille** — "when an Indian studies a tribal village he is an *anthropologist*,
  a non-tribal village a *sociologist*"; **M.N. Srinivas** — village studies bridge both; **Satish
  Deshpande & Patricia Uberoi** — the simple/complex distinction itself needs rethinking.

**↔ POLITICAL SCIENCE**
- Political science studies the **State, government, power, authority, statecraft** (organised
  political life) via **published documents, census, official reports, election surveys**; sociology
  gives **no primacy** to any one institution — it studies power in **all** contexts (family,
  university, religion) via **micro-level fieldwork**.
- **Overlap → Political Sociology.** **Weber** (ideal types of **authority**); **C.W. Mills** (*Power
  Elite*); **Robert Michels** (*Iron Law of Oligarchy*); **Robert Dahl**; **Foucault** (power/
  knowledge). **Indian:** **Rajni Kothari** — *Caste in Indian Politics* (the "politicisation of
  caste").

**↔ PHILOSOPHY**
- Philosophy seeks to understand reality **in totality** and is concerned with **ends/values**;
  sociology grew **out of** philosophy (grand theories of **Comte, the Enlightenment, Hegel's
  dialectic, Rousseau's General Will**). **Post-modernism** and **Marxism** are simultaneously
  philosophy *and* sociological theory.

## PART B — SCIENCE, SCIENTIFIC METHOD AND CRITIQUE

### B.1 What is Science?
- **Giddens:** science = the **systematic use of methods of investigation, logical analysis and
  argument to build a body of knowledge.** Two senses: a **body of knowledge** + a **method**.
- **Goals:** to **explain** why things happen, to **generalise**, to **predict**.
- **Seven features (LevelUp):** ① **Empirical** ② **Testable/falsifiable** ③ **Provisional**
  ④ **Cumulative** ⑤ **Objective** ⑥ **Replicable** ⑦ **Theoretical**. *(H.M. Johnson: sociology
  shares these to some extent.)*
- **Merton's CUDOS** (norms of the scientific ethos): **C**ommunalism, **U**niversalism,
  **D**isinterestedness, **O**riginality, **S**kepticism (e.g., Jonas Salk not patenting the polio
  vaccine = communalism).

### B.2 The Scientific Method
- **Francis Bacon** — knowledge via **observation, data, measurement, experiment** (**empiricism /
  inductive method**), against Church dogma.
- **Elements/Characteristics (Horton & Hunt):** objectivity · accuracy · precision · trained
  investigators · replicability · neutrality · verifiable evidence · systematic recording ·
  controlled conditions.
- **Classical postulates (inductivism)** — the **swan example:** observe 100 white swans → hypothesise
  "all swans are white" → test → accept/refute (one **black swan** refutes it).
- **The 8 steps (with the DINK-family example):** ① select topic → ② define research question →
  ③ review literature (find the **research gap**) → ④ formulate **hypothesis** + **operationalise
  variables** → ⑤ choose **method & sample** (representative) → ⑥ collect data → ⑦ analyse (e.g.,
  **SPSS**, correlation) & generalise → ⑧ conclude & publish. *(Hypothesis = a conjectural statement
  linking two variables.)*
- **Significance:** standardisation (e.g., **NSSO surveys**) → higher **validity & reliability**,
  **prediction**, **universal applicability**, **ethical neutrality**.

### B.3 Is Sociology a Science?  (YES → BUT → THEREFORE)
**YES — Positivist view (Comte, Durkheim):** society exists as a natural system "out there";
apply **objective, quantitative** method → observe, measure, correlate, form **laws**.
- **Durkheim's *Suicide* (1897) & *The Rules of Sociological Method* (1895)** — social facts as
  *things*; explained a "personal" act by **social facts** (integration & regulation).
- *"Positivism proved that scientific method is not romanticism but empirical reality."* — Durkheim.
  **G.C. Homans:** positivism took sociology "from nowhere to the stature of a discipline."

**Weber — still a science, but a *social* science:** because the subject matter differs, the **method**
must differ — sociology is an **interpretive science** (*Verstehen*). It remains scientific: systematic
data collection, empirical evidence (interviews), value-free analysis, logical reasoning, open to
scrutiny. → **Non-positivist methodology is scientific — but its notion of science differs.**

### B.4 The CRITIQUE — limits of the scientific method in social science
*(Adarsh's 7 + handout)*
1. **Problem of experimentation** — can't fully control social behaviour; ethically humans are **not
   guinea pigs**.
2. **Problem of measurement/quantification** — how to quantify **beauty, secularism, nationalism,
   urbanisation**? Heterogeneous social data.
3. **Problem of generalisation** — man is **volitional**, has free will & consciousness (**"I" & "Me"
   — Mead**); universal laws are hard.
4. **Problem of objectivity** — researcher is *inside* society. **Gunnar Myrdal:** "total objectivity
   is an **illusion**."
5. **Risk of reductionism** — overlooks qualitative depth (e.g., Indian rituals → symbolic meanings).
6. **Oversimplification** + **validity/reliability** problems (dishonest/socially-desirable responses,
   unrepresentative samples).
7. **Plurality of causes & intermixture of cause–effect** (poverty: many causes; is economic
   stability the cause *or* effect of childlessness?).
> **Fix:** replace lab experiments with **interview/questionnaire/field observation**, and complement
> objectivity with **inter-subjectivity & interpretation**.

### B.5 The philosophy-of-science CRITIQUE (this is the syllabus "critique" — must-cover)
- **Karl Popper — Falsificationism:** science grows by **falsification**, not verification; the
  **demarcation principle** — a claim is scientific only if **falsifiable**. Astrology and Freud's
  unconscious are **unfalsifiable** (pseudo-science); Marxism became pseudo-science when followers
  shielded it with **"false consciousness."** *(Sociology can be scientific if its theories are
  falsifiable.)*
- **Paul Feyerabend — Epistemological Anarchism:** "the worst enemy of scientific method"; there is
  **no single universal method** — scientists use intuition, hunches, myths, serendipity ("**anything
  goes**"). ~30–40% of discoveries are serendipitous (Newton, Nobel, Pasteur). → Science is **just
  another ideology**; demarcation collapses.
- **Thomas Kuhn — Paradigms:** science moves through **① pre-science → ② normal science → ③ crisis →
  ④ paradigm shift/revolution.** What counts as "science" is decided by the **scientific community**
  → **science is a social process.** Sociology has **multiple competing paradigms** (functionalism,
  Marxism, interactionism, feminism) → Kuhn calls it **pre-scientific / multi-paradigmatic.**
- **Feminist critique of the scientific method:** ① **objectivity doesn't exist** — knowledge is
  **androcentric/sexist** (man = "head of household"; only men's paid work studied); ② **over-
  generalisation** from male-only samples; ③ Darwin (*Descent of Man*) & Spencer deemed women inferior
  — **Antoinette Brown Blackwell** was the first published feminist critic of Darwin; **Dorothy Smith
  & Patricia Hill Collins** — **standpoint theory**, androcentric bias.
- **Post-positivism (Popper, Kuhn, Lakatos) / Critical Realism:** retains systematic inquiry but
  accepts that observation is **theory-laden**, complete objectivity is impossible, knowledge is
  **provisional & fallible**; uses **mixed methods**; **probabilistic** (not deterministic) causality.
  A form lying between positivism & interpretivism = **Critical Realism**.

### B.6 Objectivity & Value-Neutrality (core to "is sociology a science / critique")
- **Fact** = verifiable observation. **Value** = beliefs about what is right/desirable.
- **Value-neutrality (Positivists)** → **Value-relevance (Weber)** → **Value-laden / Value-based
  (Anti-positivists / committed sociology).**
- **Objectivity** = frame of mind where personal prejudices don't contaminate data collection or
  theory. **Radcliffe-Brown:** the social scientist "must abandon his **ethnocentric/egocentric
  bias**." **Durkheim** (*Rules*) sought value-neutrality.
- **Weber's method of maintaining objectivity:** **Value-relevance** (values may enter *topic
  choice*) BUT **Value-freedom (*Wertfreiheit*)** in the research process — separate **"what is"
  (science)** from **"what ought to be" (values)**; plus **Verstehen**, **Ideal Types**, **causal
  probabilism**, and attention to **historical/cultural context**. *(Weber: complete detachment is
  hard, but sociology must strive for it — "iron will.")*
- **The committed-sociology challenge:**
  - **Alvin Gouldner** — *"Anti-Minotaur: The Myth of a Value-Free Sociology"* → value-freedom is
    **impossible & undesirable**; be open about values = **Reflexive Sociology.**
  - **Gunnar Myrdal** — objectivity is an **illusion**; all research is guided by viewpoints → state
    value premises.
  - **Howard Becker** — "**Whose side are we on?**" → take the side of the **underdog**.
  - **Gomm** — "the very idea of value-free sociology is unsociological"; **David Marsland** —
    sociology tilts left. **Melissa/Marnia?** *(feminist:)* "classical sociology is **male-streamed**."
- **Reflexivity & the double bind:**
  - **Anthony Giddens — Double Hermeneutic:** sociologists are **part of the world they study**, and
    their concepts (e.g., "role model", "vote bank") **feed back** into society.
  - **Pierre Bourdieu** — the researcher is embedded in the social world → complete objectivity is
    hard → **reflexivity**. **Merton — confirmation bias.** **Phil Carspecken** — critical
    ethnography: the sociologist should be **committed to changing the world**.
- **Problems in maintaining objectivity (7):** ① inherent human bias ② socio-cultural background
  ③ **emotional involvement** (Ann Oakley) ④ interpretation of data (divorce-rate ↑ read as "decline
  of family" *or* "women's autonomy") ⑤ research **funding/gatekeepers** ⑥ selective recording/data
  access ⑦ **reactivity** (respondents hide/exaggerate) + **Hawthorne effect**.

### B.7 Verdict (use as conclusion)
- **Robert Bierstedt (*The Social Order*):** sociology is a **generalising**, **social** (not natural),
  and largely **pure** science.
- **Andrew Sayer:** society, like the weather, is **too complex** for exact prediction — so sociology
  is **not** a science *the way physics/chemistry* (**closed systems**) are; it is a science *the way
  **seismology & meteorology** are* (**open systems**, many variables).
- **Bottom line:** *Sociology is the **most scientific of the humanities and the most humanistic of
  the sciences.*** It **is** a science — empirical, theoretical, cumulative — but a **special social
  science** demanding both **explanation** and **interpretive understanding**; its objectivity is a
  **disciplined aspiration** secured through method, peer scrutiny and **reflexivity**.
