Part A · The Discipline

Scope of the Subject & Comparison with Other Social Sciences

A.0 Nature of Sociology (open with this)

Sociology is a scientific discipline — objective, systematic, empirical — but cannot be directly modelled on natural science, since human behaviour is conscious & volitional, unlike the world of objects. Bierstedt: sociology is generalising, a social (not natural) science, and largely pure.

A.1 – A.2 Scope & the two schools

Scope = subject-matter/boundaries. There is no single agreed scope → the classic debate:

Formalistic / Specialistic

  • Simmel, Von Wiese, Vierkandt, Tönnies, Weber, Small
  • Pure, independent, limited science
  • Studies FORMS (competition, cooperation, sub/super-ordination) abstracted from contents
  • Critique: form & content inseparable; too narrow

Synthetic

  • Durkheim, Ginsberg, Hobhouse, Sorokin
  • General, synthesising science
  • Durkheim's 3 parts: Social Morphology · Social Physiology · General Sociology
  • Critique: risks losing a distinct identity

A.3 "Goal of Sociology" grid (value-add framing)

OU of OR
Objective Understanding of Objective Reality — scope restricted to hard facts. Positivists — Durkheim, Comte.
SU of OR
Subjective Understanding of Objective Reality — value-accommodation. Weber, Symbolic Interactionism; R.K. Mukherjee.
SU of SR
Subjective Understanding of Subjective Reality. Anti-positivists — Schutz, Garfinkel.

Theory ladder: Grand/universal theories → Ideal types (Weber) → Middle-Range Theory (Merton) → concepts (e.g. Beck's Risk Society). "All sociology is contextual & ad-hoc."

A.4 Evolution of the scope

  • Initial phase (macro): Comte's Social Statics & Dynamics; inductive/deductive; Spencer's organismic analogy; humanistic Chicago School.
  • 2nd phase: Formal → micro-phenomena (Tönnies) · Informal → "study everything" (Mannheim, Sorokin) → General vs Special/Historical sociology.
  • 3rd phase — Blending: Parsons; Merton's MRT & SOSA.
  • Now — Multidisciplinary (Bryman). Howard Becker: good sociology = meaningful descriptions + valid explanations + realistic proposals.

A.5 Scope by subject-matter (recall list)

Social institutions · social control (formal; informal — ridicule, opprobrium) · social networks (formal/informal) · social pathology · social processes (associative/dissociative) · social action · social change. India: caste, village, joint family, religion, agrarian classes, movements → under globalisation: westernisation, social media, queer/environmental movements.

A.6 Comparison — 3 phases of the relationship

① Competitive beginnings (society as sui generis — Durkheim) → ② Collaborative borrowings (fieldwork ← anthropology, survey ← statistics) → ③ Interdisciplinary convergence (political/environmental sociology). Multidisciplinary = side-by-side; Interdisciplinary = integrated.

↔ History

Radcliffe-Brown: "history & sociology have no relation" — history idiographic/conjectural, sociology nomothetic/empirical (patterns vs unique events). Howard: "history is past sociology, sociology is present history." Abrams: sociology is deeply historical. E.H. Carr: the more sociological history is, the better. Historical Sociology: Ghurye (caste), A.R. Desai (nationalism), Barrington Moore (democracy & dictatorship), Weber (capitalism). Value-adds: Wallerstein (World-Systems), Halbwachs (Collective Memory), Theda Skocpol, subaltern vs colonial historiography.

↔ Economics

Economics = production–consumption–exchange; Homo economicus; quantitative. Sociology: economy is one institution; man is a social being (wealth for prestige = Veblen's conspicuous consumption; caste networks in jobs). Smith "Eco>Socio", Durkheim "Socio>Eco", Marx economic determinism, Weber PESC. Economic Sociology value-adds: Granovetter (embeddedness), Gary Becker (Human Capital), Behavioural economics/Nudge, Barbara Wootton, gender budgeting, dependency theory, CSR; Béteille (caste × economic inequality).

↔ Psychology

Psychology = individual mind/cognition/emotion (psychic facts; Freud's psychoanalysis) — personal troubles; Sociology = external social facts — public issues (suicide: why the rate varies). Radcliffe-Brown: mental vs social systems. Bridge: social psychology; Gerth & Mills — role bridges the two; Cooley ("Me"), Mead. Value-adds: C.W. Mills, Oscar Lewis (subculture of poverty), Goffman, Albert Cohen (status frustration), Judith Butler (gender acts).

↔ Social Anthropology

Kroeber: "twin sisters." Anthropology = small, simple, pre-literate, tribal societies, holistic fieldwork/ethnography (Malinowski, Evans-Pritchard/Nuer, Radcliffe-Brown/Andamanese). Sociology = modern, complex, industrial societies via surveys. Voget: difference now is of application, not scope. Value-adds: Radcliffe-Brown ("comparative sociology", nomothetic), Ghurye, Béteille ("tribal village → anthropologist; non-tribal → sociologist"), M.N. Srinivas (village studies bridge both), Deshpande & Uberoi.

↔ Political Science

PS = State, government, power, authority, statecraft (documents, census, elections); Sociology gives no primacy to one institution — power in family/religion/university via micro fieldwork. Overlap → Political Sociology: Weber (authority), C.W. Mills (Power Elite), Michels (Iron Law of Oligarchy), Dahl, Foucault. Indian: Rajni Kothari — Caste in Indian Politics.

↔ Philosophy

Philosophy = reality in totality, concerned with ends/values; sociology grew out of philosophy (Comte, Enlightenment, Hegel's dialectic, Rousseau's General Will). Post-modernism & Marxism = philosophy + sociological theory.

Part B · Sociology as Science

Science, Scientific Method & Critique

B.1 What is science?

Giddens: science = the systematic use of method & logical argument to build a body of knowledge (a body of knowledge + a method). Goals: explain · generalise · predict.

7 features: empirical · testable/falsifiable · provisional · cumulative · objective · replicable · theoretical. Merton's CUDOS: Communalism · Universalism · Disinterestedness · Originality · Skepticism (Salk not patenting the polio vaccine).

B.2 The scientific method

Bacon — observation, data, measurement, experiment (empiricism/induction). Characteristics (Horton & Hunt): objectivity, accuracy, precision, trained investigators, replicability, neutrality. Classical swan postulate; one black swan refutes.

1 · Topic2 · Research Q3 · Literature/gap4 · Hypothesis + operationalise5 · Method & sample6 · Collect data7 · Analyse (SPSS) + generalise8 · Conclude & publish

Standardisation (e.g. NSSO) → validity & reliability, prediction, universal applicability, ethical neutrality.

B.3 Is Sociology a Science? (YES → BUT → THEREFORE)

YES — Positivist (Comte, Durkheim)

  • Society = natural system "out there"; objective, quantitative method → laws
  • Durkheim's Suicide (1897) & Rules (1895) — social facts as things
  • "Positivism = not romanticism but empirical reality" (Durkheim); Homans: took sociology from nowhere to a discipline

Weber — still a science, but a special social science: subject matter differs → method differs (Verstehen). Non-positivist methodology is scientific (systematic, empirical via interviews, value-free, logical, open to scrutiny).

B.4 CRITIQUE — limits of scientific method in social science

Seven limitations

  • Experimentation — no control; humans are not guinea pigs
  • Measurement — quantify beauty, secularism, nationalism?
  • Generalisation — man is volitional; free will & consciousness ("I" & "Me" — Mead)
  • Objectivity — Myrdal: "total objectivity is an illusion"
  • Reductionism — overlooks qualitative depth (Indian rituals → symbolic meanings)
  • Oversimplification + validity/reliability (dishonest/desirable responses)
  • Plurality of causes & cause–effect intermixture

Fix: lab → interview/questionnaire/field observation; objectivity complemented by inter-subjectivity & interpretation.

B.5 Philosophy-of-science CRITIQUE (the syllabus "critique")

Karl Popper — Falsificationism
Science grows by falsification, not verification; demarcation — scientific = falsifiable. Astrology/Freud = unfalsifiable; Marxism → pseudo-science via "false consciousness."
Paul Feyerabend — Epistemological Anarchism
"Worst enemy of method"; no single method — intuition, hunches, serendipity → "anything goes"; science is just another ideology.
Thomas Kuhn — Paradigms
Pre-science → normal science → crisis → paradigm shift. Community decides "science" → science is a social process; sociology is multi-paradigmatic / pre-scientific.
Feminist critique
Objectivity doesn't exist — androcentric/sexist bias; over-generalisation from male samples; Antoinette Brown Blackwell vs Darwin; D. Smith & P.H. Collins — standpoint theory.
Post-positivism / Critical Realism
Observation is theory-laden; knowledge is provisional & fallible; mixed methods; probabilistic causality. Critical Realism sits between positivism & interpretivism.

B.6 Objectivity & Value-Neutrality

  Comte, Durkheim)    topic choice only)    Gouldner, Myrdal, Becker)

Weber's method: value-relevance in topic choice BUT value-freedom (Wertfreiheit) in the process — separate "what is" from "what ought"; plus Verstehen, ideal types, causal probabilism, historical context. Radcliffe-Brown: abandon ethnocentric/egocentric bias.

The committed-sociology challenge

  • Gouldner — "Anti-Minotaur": value-freedom is a myth → Reflexive Sociology
  • Myrdal — objectivity is an illusion → state value premises
  • Becker — "Whose side are we on?" → the underdog
  • Gomm, Marsland; feminists — "classical sociology is male-streamed"

Reflexivity & the double bind

  • Giddens — Double Hermeneutic: sociologist is part of the world; concepts feed back ("role model", "vote bank")
  • Bourdieu — researcher embedded → reflexivity
  • Merton — confirmation bias; Carspecken — critical ethnography

Problems maintaining objectivity: inherent bias · socio-cultural background · emotional involvement (Ann Oakley) · interpretation of data · funding/gatekeepers · selective recording · reactivity + Hawthorne effect.

B.7 Verdict (conclusion)

Bierstedt: generalising, social, pure science. Sayer: society is too complex for exact prediction → sociology is a science not like physics/chemistry (closed systems) but like seismology & meteorology (open systems). Bottom line — sociology is the most scientific of the humanities and the most humanistic of the sciences: a special social science needing both explanation and interpretive understanding; objectivity is a disciplined aspiration via method, peer scrutiny & reflexivity.
