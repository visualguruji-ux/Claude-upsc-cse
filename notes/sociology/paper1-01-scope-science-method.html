Part A · The Discipline

Scope of the Subject & Comparison with Other Social Sciences

A.1 What is Sociology?

Etymology: socius (Latin, companion) + logos (Greek, study) → "science of society." Coined by Auguste Comte, 1839 — first called "social physics," then renamed after A. Quetelet used that term.

Comte
Science of social order (statics) & progress (dynamics).
Durkheim
Science of social institutions; study of social facts.
Max Weber
Interpretive understanding (Verstehen) of social action.
Ginsberg
Study of human interactions & inter-relations, their conditions & consequences.

A.2 Scope — the two schools

Formalistic / Specialistic

  • Simmel, Von Wiese, Vierkandt, Tönnies, Weber, Small
  • Pure, independent, limited science
  • Studies FORMS of relations (competition, cooperation, sub/super-ordination) abstracted from contents
  • Critique: form & content inseparable; too narrow

Synthetic

  • Durkheim, Ginsberg, Hobhouse, Sorokin
  • General, synthesising science of society
  • Durkheim's 3 parts: Social Morphology · Social Physiology · General Sociology
  • Critique: risks losing a distinct identity
Balanced view: Sociology = a general science of society with its own perspective — it studies society as a whole & as a system of relationships, while other social sciences study particular segments. Scope is wide but distinctive.

A.3 Nature of Sociology (Bierstedt)

Independent · a social (not natural) science · categorical ("what is") not normative · pure not applied · relatively abstract · generalising · a general social science · both rational & empirical.

A.4 Comparison with other social sciences

↔ Anthropology — "twin sisters" (Kroeber)
Anthropology: small, simple, pre-literate, homogeneous, tribal societies studied holistically via fieldwork/ethnography. Sociology: large, complex, modern, heterogeneous societies via surveys/statistics. Line is blurring — e.g. M.N. Srinivas' village studies.
↔ Political Science
PS = State, government, power, formal politics (man as political being). Sociology = all social relations incl. political. Overlap → Political Sociology. Sociology is broader.
↔ Economics
Economics = production–distribution–exchange–consumption, one specialised aspect; precise & quantitative (ceteris paribus). Sociology sees economic life as embedded in social life → Economic Sociology (Weber's Protestant Ethic, Marx's base–superstructure, Veblen).
↔ History
History = concrete, unique past events (particularising). Sociology = recurrent patterns & general laws (generalising). G.E. Howard: "History is past sociology; sociology is present history." → Historical Sociology.
↔ Psychology
Psychology = the individual mind & behaviour; Sociology = group/collective behaviour. Bridge = Social Psychology (G.H. Mead). Durkheim: social facts are sui generis — explained by other social facts, not psychology.
Memory hook: Economics, Political Science, History study slices of social reality; Sociology studies the whole loaf — and how the slices interconnect.
Part B · Sociology as Science

Science, Scientific Method & Critique

B.1 What is "science"?

Two meanings: a body of systematic, verified knowledge + a method/approach. Attributes: empiricism · objectivity · value-neutrality · verifiability · cause–effect · generalisation · prediction · quantification · reliability & validity.

1 · Observation2 · Hypothesis3 · Data collection 4 · Analysis/testing5 · Generalisation/theory6 · Prediction7 · Verification/falsification

B.2 The case FOR — Positivist tradition

  • Comte (Positivism): society ruled by invariable natural laws; Law of Three Stages — Theological → Metaphysical → Positive.
  • Durkheim — Rules of Sociological Method (1895): treat social facts as things (external · constraining · general); study objectively.
  • Durkheim — Suicide (1897): statistics + comparative method + correlation → linked suicide rates to integration & regulation (egoistic/altruistic/anomic/fatalistic). Explained a "personal" act by social facts.
  • Tools sociology does use: observation, comparative & historical method, surveys, sampling, statistics, (limited) experiments.

B.3 The CRITIQUE — why it isn't natural science

Seven limitations

  • Objectivity — researcher is inside society; bias of value/class/gender
  • Experimentation — no controlled lab; ethical limits; humans not passive
  • Prediction — free will & reflexivity; self-fulfilling prophecy (Merton)
  • Generalisation — societies vary by time, space, culture
  • Measurement — attitudes/values lack natural units
  • Hawthorne Effect — the observed change their behaviour
  • Distinct subject — humans have consciousness & meaning (Weber)

B.4 The interpretive / anti-positivist alternative

  • Weber — Verstehen: social action carries subjective meaning; combine interpretive understanding with causal explanation. Sociology = a special social science. Tools: Ideal type, value-relevance + value-freedom.
  • Non-positivist methods (syllabus): Phenomenology (Schutz) · Ethnomethodology (Garfinkel) · Symbolic Interactionism (Mead, Blumer) · Social Constructionism (Berger & Luckmann, 1966).
  • Anti-naturalism: society ≠ nature → natural-science method inappropriate.

B.5 Post-positivist & critical challenges

  • Popper — Falsificationism: science advances by falsifying, not verifying.
  • Kuhn — Paradigms: science is community/paradigm-bound; sociology is multi-paradigmatic.
  • Critical Theory (Habermas): positivism serves the status quo; three knowledge-interests (technical / practical / emancipatory).
  • Feminist critique: mainstream = "malestream"; Standpoint theory (D. Smith, S. Harding).
  • Postmodernism (Lyotard, Foucault): rejects grand narratives & universal truth.

B.6 Fact, Value & Objectivity

Weber
Value-free in analysis, but value-relevant in choosing the problem. Objectivity = rigorous method free of bias; value-neutrality = own preferences don't distort findings.
Myrdal
A fully value-free sociology is a myth → state your value-premises explicitly.
Gouldner
"Anti-Minotaur: The Myth of a Value-Free Sociology" — value-freedom itself became an ideology.
Verdict: Sociology is a science — systematic, empirical, self-correcting, striving for objectivity — but a social science, not a copy of physics. Its subject (conscious, meaning-making humans) needs both explanation and understanding. It is multi-paradigmatic; objectivity is a disciplined aspiration via method, peer scrutiny & reflexivity.
