# ARTICLE 12 — DEFINITION OF "STATE"
### Class handout · companion to the 19-slide deck
**Part III — Fundamental Rights · Articles 12–35 · GS Paper II (Polity & Governance)**

---

## [S2] WHY ARTICLE 12 DECIDES EVERYTHING

- Fundamental Rights bind the **State** — not private individuals (general rule).
- ∴ Art. 12 answers: **whom can you sue** for an FR violation?
- It fixes the reach of **Art. 32** (SC writ) and, indirectly, **Art. 226** (HC writ).
- **Art. 36** → "State" in **Part IV (DPSP)** carries the *same* meaning.

> **THE ESCAPE PROBLEM.** If only the Union and States were bound, the government could
> violate rights *through a corporation it owns* and claim immunity. Article 12 exists to
> shut that door — **the State cannot escape FRs by changing its clothes.**

## [S3] THE PROVISION

> "In this Part, **unless the context otherwise requires**, 'the State' **includes** the
> Government and Parliament of India, the Government and the Legislature of each of the
> States, and all **local or other authorities** within the territory of India **or** under
> the control of the Government of India."

**Four limbs:** ① Govt + Parliament of India · ② Govt + Legislature of each State ·
③ **All local authorities** ④ **Other authorities**

## [S4] THREE WORDS THAT CARRY MARKS

**① "includes"** → the definition is **INCLUSIVE, not exhaustive**; courts can add to it.
  ✗ *Trap:* "Article 12 exhaustively defines the State."

**② "unless the context otherwise requires"** → built-in flexibility; the same body may be
  "State" for one purpose and not another. *(Explains why answers are fact-specific.)*

**③ "…OR under the control of the Government of India"** → the **"or" is DISJUNCTIVE**;
  a body **outside** India's territory but under GoI control **is still State**.
  ✗ *Trap:* "It must be within Indian territory." 

## [S5] FRAMERS' INTENT — the Mains opener

> "…every authority which has got either the **power to make laws** or the **power to have
> discretion vested in it**." — **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, Constituent Assembly, 25 Nov 1948**

➡️ The intellectual **seed of the instrumentality / agency doctrine.**

## [S6] TWO BRANCHES — TWO DIFFERENT TESTS *(never merge them)*

- **Branch 1 — LOCAL AUTHORITIES** → test: **R.C. Jain (1981)** → **LAAFS**
- **Branch 2 — OTHER AUTHORITIES** → tests: **Rajasthan SEB (1967) → Ajay Hasia (1981) → Pradeep Kumar Biswas (2002)**

---

# BRANCH 1 — LOCAL AUTHORITIES

## [S7] *Union of India v. R.C. Jain* (1981) — the DDA case

**Anchor:** Constitution does not define "local authority" → **S. 3(31), General Clauses Act 1897**
→ imported by **Art. 367(1)** ⭐

**Five attributes — L A A F S**

- **L** — separate **L**egal existence → body corporate, not a mere govt agency/dept
- **A** — functions in a defined **A**rea → *"…and **ordinarily** elected by the inhabitants"*
- **A** — degree of **A**utonomy → an "appreciable measure"; need not be complete
- **F** — power to raise **F**unds → taxes, rates, charges, fees; **control of the fund must vest in it**
- **S** — entrusted by **S**tatute with municipal-type functions → health, education, water,
  sewerage, town planning, roads, markets, transport, welfare

## [S8] THE NUANCE EXAMINERS BUILD QUESTIONS ON

⚠️ **"ORDINARILY"** → election is a **strong indicator, NOT a mandatory condition.**
That is exactly why the **non-elected DDA** was still held a local authority.

✗ **TRAP:** *"An elected body is essential for local-authority status."* → **FALSE**

**Examples:** Municipalities · Panchayats · District Boards · Improvement Trusts ·
Port Commissioners · Town/Notified Area Committees · Development Authorities (DDA) · Mining Boards

# BRANCH 2 — "OTHER AUTHORITIES"

## [S9] THE CORE QUESTION

Article 12 never defines the phrase. Every case since 1954 answers: **"Who ELSE counts as the State?"**

⭐ **THE SHIFT:** courts moved from asking **"HOW was this body born?"** → to asking
**"WHO really controls it?"**

## [S10] PHASE 1 (1954) — narrow view · ✗ REJECTED

***University of Madras v. Santa Bai*** — **Madras HIGH COURT**, not the SC
- Applied ***ejusdem generis*** = "of the same kind"
  *(plain example: "dogs, cats and other animals" = **pets**, not lions)*
- ∴ only bodies doing **governmental/sovereign functions** = State; a university merely teaches → not State
- ✗ **NO LONGER GOOD LAW**

## [S10] PHASE 2 (1962 & 1967) — *ejusdem generis* rejected

- ***Ujjam Bai*** (1962) — words of **"wide amplitude"**
- ⭐ ***Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal*** (1967) — **NO COMMON GENUS** runs through the Art. 12 list
  (Parliament, Government, Municipality share no common kind) → the rule collapses
- **New rule:** every authority **created by the Constitution or by a statute**, on whom powers
  are conferred by law, is "State"
- ⭐ **Need NOT perform sovereign functions — may even be purely COMMERCIAL**

## [S11] PHASE 3 (1975–81) — agency / instrumentality

*Sukhdev Singh* (1975) ONGC, LIC, IFCI · *R.D. Shetty* (1979) 5 tests ·
*Som Prakash Rekhi* (1981) govt company · ⭐ ***Ajay Hasia*** (1981) — **6 tests**

1. **Who OWNS it?** — share capital held by the Government
2. **Who PAYS its bills?** — State assistance meets almost the **entire** expenditure
3. **Who PROTECTS it?** — State-conferred / State-protected **monopoly** status
4. **Who RUNS it daily?** — **deep and pervasive** State control
5. **What does it DO?** — functions of **public importance**, closely related to govt functions
6. **Where did it COME FROM?** — a **department of Government transferred** to it

**Mnemonic: OWNS · PAYS · PROTECTS · RUNS · DOES · CAME FROM**

⭐ Not a checklist — weighed together. **FORM is irrelevant; SUBSTANCE of control decides.**

## [S12] PHASE 4 (2002) — the final word

***Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. IICB*** — **7-judge bench · 5:2 · CSIR held to be "State"**
→ **overruled *Sabhajit Tewary* (1975)**

**TEST:** body must be **financially + functionally + administratively DOMINATED** by the
Government, and that control must be **(a) "deep and pervasive"** and **(b) particular to that body.**

⚠️ **Mere government funding, patronage or regulation is NOT enough.**
*Ask: is the government the real **boss**, or only a **donor and regulator**?*

**Why CSIR "flipped":** 1975 ✗ → 2002 ✓. **CSIR did not change — the TEST changed.**
Only a larger (7-judge) bench could overrule the earlier 5-judge decision.

## [S13] IS THE JUDICIARY "STATE"?

**✗ NOT State — JUDICIAL functions** (hearing cases, delivering judgments)
*Rationale: protect judicial independence.*
- *Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar* (1966), **9 judges** — a judicial order cannot be challenged under
  Art. 32; **remedy = appeal**
- *Rupa Ashok Hurra* (2002) — reaffirmed; also the **CURATIVE PETITION** case

**✓ IS State — ADMINISTRATIVE / RULE-MAKING functions**
Appointments, service matters, rules under Arts. 145 & 227.

🧠 **Judging → NOT State. Managing → State.**
✗ **TRAP:** *"The judiciary is never State."* → **FALSE**

## [S14] SCORECARD

**✓ STATE:** Rajasthan SEB (1967) · ONGC, LIC, IFCI (1975) · Intl. Airport Authority (1979) ·
Society running REC (Ajay Hasia 1981) · Bharat Petroleum (Som Prakash Rekhi 1981) ·
DDA (R.C. Jain 1981) · **CSIR (2002)** · Municipalities, Panchayats, District Boards

**✗ NOT STATE:** University of Madras (1954, *reasoning rejected*) · **CSIR (Sabhajit Tewary 1975 — OVERRULED)** ·
NCERT (1991) · BCCI (2005) · Judiciary in judicial functions

⚠️ **Favourite trap:** CSIR appears on **both** lists. Since 2002 it **IS** State.

## [S15] TWO "NO" CASES — AND THE REMEDY THAT SURVIVES

**NCERT ✗** — *Chander Mohan Khanna* (1991): "largely an **autonomous body**"; activities not
wholly related to govt functions; control confined to **proper utilisation of the grant** (a donor
checking receipts, not a boss).
> ⭐ *"A wide enlargement of the meaning must be tempered by a **wise limitation**."*

**BCCI ✗** — *Zee Telefilms* (2005): no statute; no share capital; no substantial financial
assistance; monopoly is **de facto**, never State-conferred or protected.
*(2015 — BCCI v. Cricket Assn. of Bihar → **Lodha Committee**.)*

> ⭐⭐ **BUT BCCI performs a PUBLIC FUNCTION** (selecting the national team) → **writ lies under Art. 226.**
> **Art. 32** → only against "State". **Art. 226** → also against a **private body performing a public duty**
> (*Andi Mukta*, 1989). **∴ Article 226 is WIDER than Article 32.**

## [S16] LATEST SHIFT — *Kaushal Kishor v. State of U.P.* (2023)

**5-judge Constitution Bench · 4:1 · 3 Jan 2023**
- FRs under **Arts. 19 and 21** enforceable even against **PRIVATE / NON-STATE ACTORS**
- Grounds of restriction in **Art. 19(2) are exhaustive**
- ⭐ **Dilutes Art. 12's gatekeeping** — the old binary (State ⇒ rights apply; private ⇒ they don't) is breaking down

**Already horizontal:** Art. 15(2) shops/hotels/wells/roads · Art. 17 untouchability ·
Art. 23 trafficking/*begar* · Art. 24 child labour

## [S18] STATEMENT-BASED TRAPS

**✗ FALSE:** "other authorities" read *ejusdem generis* · Art. 12 is exhaustive · all PSUs
automatically State · judiciary never State · BCCI is State · NCERT is State · elected body
essential for local authority · mere govt aid ⇒ State

**✓ TRUE:** definition is inclusive · governs Part III **and** Part IV · body outside India under GoI
control is State · body need not be sovereign, may be commercial · CSIR is State (2002) ·
Art. 226 wider than Art. 32 · Arts. 19 & 21 partly horizontal (2023)

## [S19] MAINS ARGUMENT (GS-II)

> **Thesis:** the trend is from an **INSTITUTIONAL** test to a **FUNCTIONAL** one —
> ***what* is being done, not *who* is doing it.**

**Framings:** (i) "Other authorities" judicially expanded beyond the framers' contemplation — examine.
(ii) Art. 12 is the gatekeeper of Part III — in an era of privatisation and PPP, is that gate too narrow?
(iii) Should the judiciary be brought within "State"?

**Body arc:** restrictive (*Santa Bai*) → rejection (*Ujjam Bai*, *Rajasthan SEB*) → instrumentality
(*Shetty*, *Ajay Hasia*) → consolidation **and a deliberate brake** (*P.K. Biswas*; *NCERT*'s
"wise limitation") → contemporary gap (PPP, outsourced public services) → judicial answer
(Art. 226 public-function test; *Kaushal Kishor* horizontality).

**Value additions:** Ambedkar (CAD 1948) · NCERT "wise limitation" · P.K. Biswas formula ·
Kaushal Kishor 2023 · **comparative:** US "state action" doctrine; South Africa expressly binds
private persons to the Bill of Rights.
