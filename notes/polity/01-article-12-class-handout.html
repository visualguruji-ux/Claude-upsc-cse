Article 12 — Definition of “State”

Part III — Fundamental Rights · Articles 12–35 · GS Paper II (Polity & Governance)

S2Why Article 12 Decides Everything

  • Fundamental Rights bind the State — not private individuals (general rule).
  • ∴ Art. 12 answers: whom can you sue for an FR violation?
  • Fixes the reach of Art. 32 (SC writ) and, indirectly, Art. 226 (HC writ).
  • Art. 36 → “State” in Part IV (DPSP) carries the same meaning.
The escape problem If only the Union and States were bound, the government could violate rights through a corporation it owns and claim immunity. Article 12 exists to shut that door — the State cannot escape FRs by changing its clothes.

S3The Provision

“In this Part, unless the context otherwise requires, ‘the State’ includes the Government and Parliament of India, the Government and the Legislature of each of the States, and all local or other authorities within the territory of India or under the control of the Government of India.”

Four limbs: ① Govt + Parliament of India · ② Govt + Legislature of each State · ③ All local authorities · ④ Other authorities

S4Three Words That Carry Marks

① “includes” → the definition is INCLUSIVE, not exhaustive; courts can add to it.
✗ Trap: “Article 12 exhaustively defines the State.”

② “unless the context otherwise requires” → built-in flexibility; the same body may be “State” for one purpose and not another. (Explains why answers are fact-specific.)

③ “…OR under the control of the Government of India” → the “or” is DISJUNCTIVE; a body outside India’s territory but under GoI control is still State.
✗ Trap: “It must be within Indian territory.”

S5Framers’ Intent — the Mains Opener

“…every authority which has got either the power to make laws or the power to have discretion vested in it.”
— Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, Constituent Assembly, 25 Nov 1948

➡️ The intellectual seed of the instrumentality / agency doctrine.

S6Two Branches — Two Different Tests (never merge them)

  • Branch 1 — LOCAL AUTHORITIES → test: R.C. Jain (1981) → LAAFS
  • Branch 2 — OTHER AUTHORITIES → Rajasthan SEB (1967) → Ajay Hasia (1981) → Pradeep Kumar Biswas (2002)
BRANCH 1 — LOCAL AUTHORITIES

S7Union of India v. R.C. Jain (1981) — the DDA case

Anchor: Constitution does not define “local authority” → S. 3(31), General Clauses Act 1897 → imported by Art. 367(1) ⭐

Five attributes — L A A F S

Lseparate Legal existence → body corporate, not a mere govt agency/dept
Afunctions in a defined Area → “…and ordinarily elected by the inhabitants”
Adegree of Autonomy → an “appreciable measure”; need not be complete
Fpower to raise Funds → taxes, rates, charges, fees; control of the fund must vest in it
Sentrusted by Statute with municipal-type functions → health, education, water, sewerage, town planning, roads, markets, transport, welfare

S8The Nuance Examiners Build Questions On

⚠ The word “ordinarily” Election is a strong indicator, NOT a mandatory condition. That is exactly why the non-elected DDA was still held a local authority.
✗ TRAP: “An elected body is essential for local-authority status.” → FALSE

Examples: Municipalities · Panchayats · District Boards · Improvement Trusts · Port Commissioners · Town/Notified Area Committees · Development Authorities (DDA) · Mining Boards

BRANCH 2 — “OTHER AUTHORITIES”

S9The Core Question

Article 12 never defines the phrase. Every case since 1954 answers: “Who ELSE counts as the State?”

⭐ The shift Courts moved from asking “HOW was this body born?” → to asking “WHO really controls it?”

S10Phase 1 (1954) — Narrow View · ✗ REJECTED

University of Madras v. Santa Bai — Madras HIGH COURT, not the Supreme Court

  • Applied ejusdem generis = “of the same kind”
    plain example: “dogs, cats and other animals” = pets, not lions
  • ∴ only bodies doing governmental / sovereign functions = State; a university merely teaches → not State
  • ✗ NO LONGER GOOD LAW

S10Phase 2 (1962 & 1967) — Ejusdem Generis Rejected

  • Ujjam Bai (1962) — words of “wide amplitude”
  • ⭐ Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal (1967) — NO COMMON GENUS runs through the Art. 12 list (Parliament, Government, Municipality share no common kind) → the rule collapses
  • New rule: every authority created by the Constitution or by a statute, on whom powers are conferred by law, is “State”
  • ⭐ Need NOT perform sovereign functions — may even be purely COMMERCIAL

S11Phase 3 (1975–81) — Agency / Instrumentality

Sukhdev Singh (1975) ONGC, LIC, IFCI · R.D. Shetty (1979) 5 tests · Som Prakash Rekhi (1981) govt company · ⭐ Ajay Hasia (1981) — six tests

  1. Who OWNS it? — share capital held by the Government
  2. Who PAYS its bills? — State assistance meets almost the entire expenditure
  3. Who PROTECTS it? — State-conferred / State-protected monopoly status
  4. Who RUNS it daily? — deep and pervasive State control
  5. What does it DO? — functions of public importance, closely related to govt functions
  6. Where did it COME FROM? — a department of Government transferred to it
🧠 OWNS · PAYS · PROTECTS · RUNS · DOES · CAME FROM
⭐ Not a checklist — weighed together. FORM is irrelevant; the SUBSTANCE of control decides.

S12Phase 4 (2002) — The Final Word

Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. IICB — 7-judge bench · 5:2 · CSIR held to be “State” → overruled Sabhajit Tewary (1975)

The test The body must be financially + functionally + administratively DOMINATED by the Government, and that control must be (a) “deep and pervasive” and (b) particular to that body.
⚠ The line that decides most questions Mere government funding, patronage or regulation is NOT enough.
Ask: is the government the real boss, or only a donor and regulator?

Why CSIR “flipped”: 1975 ✗ → 2002 ✓. CSIR did not change — the TEST changed. Only a larger (7-judge) bench could overrule the earlier 5-judge decision.

S13Is the Judiciary “State”?

✗ NOT State — JUDICIAL functions (hearing cases, delivering judgments). Rationale: protect judicial independence.
  • Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar (1966), 9 judges — a judicial order cannot be challenged under Art. 32; remedy = appeal
  • Rupa Ashok Hurra (2002) — reaffirmed; also the CURATIVE PETITION case
✓ IS State — ADMINISTRATIVE / RULE-MAKING functions — appointments, service matters, rules under Arts. 145 & 227.
🧠 Judging → NOT State. Managing → State.

✗ TRAP: “The judiciary is never State.” → FALSE

S14Scorecard

✓ STATE: Rajasthan SEB (1967) · ONGC, LIC, IFCI (1975) · Intl. Airport Authority (1979) · Society running REC (Ajay Hasia 1981) · Bharat Petroleum (Som Prakash Rekhi 1981) · DDA (R.C. Jain 1981) · CSIR (2002) · Municipalities, Panchayats, District Boards
✗ NOT STATE: University of Madras (1954, reasoning rejected) · CSIR (Sabhajit Tewary 1975 — OVERRULED) · NCERT (1991) · BCCI (2005) · Judiciary in judicial functions
⚠️ Favourite trap: CSIR appears on both lists. Since 2002 it IS State.

S15Two “No” Cases — and the Remedy That Survives

NCERT ✗ — Chander Mohan Khanna (1991): “largely an autonomous body”; activities not wholly related to govt functions; control confined to proper utilisation of the grant (a donor checking receipts, not a boss).

⭐ “A wide enlargement of the meaning must be tempered by a wise limitation.”

BCCI ✗ — Zee Telefilms (2005): no statute; no share capital; no substantial financial assistance; monopoly is de facto, never State-conferred or protected. (2015 — BCCI v. Cricket Assn. of Bihar → Lodha Committee.)

⭐⭐ The half coaching notes cut off BCCI performs a PUBLIC FUNCTION (selecting the national team) → a writ lies under Art. 226.
Art. 32 → only against “State”. Art. 226 → also against a private body performing a public duty (Andi Mukta, 1989).
∴ Article 226 is WIDER than Article 32.

S16Latest Shift — Kaushal Kishor v. State of U.P. (2023)

5-judge Constitution Bench · 4 : 1 · 3 January 2023

  • FRs under Articles 19 and 21 enforceable even against PRIVATE / NON-STATE ACTORS
  • Grounds of restriction in Art. 19(2) are exhaustive
  • ⭐ Dilutes Art. 12’s gatekeeping — the old binary (State ⇒ rights apply; private ⇒ they don’t) is breaking down

Already horizontal: Art. 15(2) shops/hotels/wells/roads · Art. 17 untouchability · Art. 23 trafficking & begar · Art. 24 child labour

S17Chronology

1954 Santa Bai (HC) ✗ · 1962 Ujjam Bai · 1966 Mirajkar (9-J) · ⭐1967 Rajasthan SEB · 1975 Sukhdev Singh ✓ | Sabhajit Tewary ✗ · 1979 R.D. Shetty · ⭐1981 Ajay Hasia · R.C. Jain · 1991 NCERT · ⭐2002 P.K. Biswas (7-J) · Rupa Ashok Hurra · 2005 Zee Telefilms · 2015 Lodha Cttee · ⭐2023 Kaushal Kishor
The four load-bearing years: 1967 · 1981 · 2002 · 2023

S18Statement-Based Traps

✗ FALSE: “other authorities” read ejusdem generis · Art. 12 is exhaustive · all PSUs automatically State · judiciary never State · BCCI is State · NCERT is State · elected body essential for local authority · mere govt aid ⇒ State
✓ TRUE: definition is inclusive · governs Part III and Part IV · body outside India under GoI control is State · body need not be sovereign, may be commercial · CSIR is State (2002) · Art. 226 wider than Art. 32 · Arts. 19 & 21 partly horizontal (2023)

S19Mains Argument (GS-II)

Thesis The trend is from an INSTITUTIONAL test to a FUNCTIONAL one — what is being done, not who is doing it.

Framings: (i) “Other authorities” judicially expanded beyond the framers’ contemplation — examine. (ii) Art. 12 is the gatekeeper of Part III — in an era of privatisation and PPP, is that gate too narrow? (iii) Should the judiciary be brought within “State”?

Body arc: restrictive (Santa Bai) → rejection (Ujjam Bai, Rajasthan SEB) → instrumentality (Shetty, Ajay Hasia) → consolidation and a deliberate brake (P.K. Biswas; NCERT’s “wise limitation”) → contemporary gap (PPP, outsourced public services) → judicial answer (Art. 226 public-function test; Kaushal Kishor horizontality).

Value additions: Ambedkar (CAD 1948) · NCERT “wise limitation” · P.K. Biswas formula · Kaushal Kishor 2023 · comparative: US “state action” doctrine; South Africa expressly binds private persons to the Bill of Rights.
