# Article 18 — Abolition of Titles

**Part III · Right to Equality (Arts. 14–18) · GS-II (Polity & Constitution)**
Sources: Bare Act · Laxmikanth (Indian Polity) · DD Basu · *Balaji Raghavan v. UoI* (1996) · Vajiram/Insights notes

---

## 1. Why Article 18 sits under "Right to Equality"

- Titles create **artificial distinctions of status** among citizens — the antithesis of equality
  before law (Art. 14).
- Colonial titles — *Rai Bahadur, Khan Bahadur, Rai Sahib, Dewan Bahadur, Knighthood (Sir)* —
  were badges of loyalty to the Crown, so the Constituent Assembly abolished them.
- Note the character of Art. 18: it is **not a "right" a citizen exercises** — it is an
  **injunction/duty**. That is why the heading says *"Abolition"*, not *"Freedom from"*.

---

## 2. The four clauses — learn them clause-by-clause

### Article 18(1)
> "No title, not being a military or academic distinction, shall be conferred by the State."

- Binds: **the State**
- Bars: conferring titles on **anyone** — citizen *or* foreigner
- **Saved:** military distinctions (Param Vir Chakra, Mahavir Chakra, Vir Chakra) and
  academic distinctions (Doctor, Professor, degrees conferred by universities)

### Article 18(2)  ← the clause most tested
> "No citizen of India shall accept any title from any foreign State."

- Binds: **every citizen of India**
- **ABSOLUTE.** No exception. **No "consent of the President" route.**
- Applies whether or not the citizen holds any office under the State.

### Article 18(3)
> "No person who is not a citizen of India shall, while he holds any office of profit or trust
> under the State, accept without the consent of the President any title from any foreign State."

- Binds: **non-citizens** *holding an office of profit or trust under the State*
- **Conditional** — permitted **with the President's consent**

### Article 18(4)
> "No person holding any office of profit or trust under the State shall, without the consent of
> the President, accept any present, emolument, or office of any kind from or under any
> foreign State."

- Binds: **any person** (citizen or not) holding an office of profit/trust
- Subject-matter is **not titles** — it is **present, emolument, or office**
- **Conditional** — permitted **with the President's consent**

---

## 3. The one distinction that decides most MCQs

- **Presidential consent exists ONLY in clauses (3) and (4).**
- **Clause (2) has NO exception** — a citizen can never accept a foreign title, period.
- Clause (1) = **State confers** · Clauses (2)–(4) = **person accepts**.
- Military/academic saving in **(1) only** — it is not repeated in (2), (3) or (4).

**Standard flips used by setters:**
1. "A citizen may accept a foreign title **with the President's consent**." → **FALSE** (that is 18(3), for non-citizens).
2. "Art. 18 bars academic distinctions." → **FALSE** (expressly saved in 18(1)).
3. "18(4) bars foreign **titles** for office-holders." → **FALSE** (18(4) covers **present, emolument or office**).
4. "Only citizens are covered by Art. 18." → **FALSE** (18(3) and 18(4) reach non-citizens too).

## 4. National awards — *Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India* (1996)

- **Challenge:** Bharat Ratna and the Padma awards are "titles" and violate Art. 18.
- **Held (5-judge Constitution Bench):** they are **NOT titles** under Art. 18. They are
  **honorary recognitions of merit/excellence**, conferring **no hereditary, social or
  political privilege**, and Art. 51A(j) (duty to strive towards excellence) supports the State
  recognising outstanding service.
- **Caveat laid down:** the awards **must NOT be used as prefixes or suffixes** to the
  recipient's name; misuse can invite forfeiture.
- Also directed: awards be conferred **sparingly** on recommendation of a high-level committee.

### Timeline of the Padma awards
- **1954** — Bharat Ratna and Padma awards instituted.
- **1977–1980** — discontinued (Janata Party government).
- **1992–1995** — suspended again during the litigation.
- **1996** — revived after *Balaji Raghavan*.

## 5. Enforcement gap (a favourite value-addition for Mains)

- Article 18 prescribes **no penalty** for its violation.
- The Constitution leaves it to **Parliament to legislate** punishment — **no such law has been
  enacted to date**. Enforcement is therefore largely **moral/administrative**.
- "Title" is **undefined** in the Constitution — the courts read it as a designation implying
  **rank, honour or privilege attached to the name**, distinguishing it from a mere award,
  decoration, degree or prize (Nobel, Magsaysay, honorary doctorates are awards, not titles).

## 6. Quick recall grid (vertical — screenshot-friendly)

**Who is bound?**
- 18(1) → the State
- 18(2) → every citizen
- 18(3) → non-citizens holding office of profit/trust
- 18(4) → any person holding office of profit/trust

**What is barred?**
- 18(1) → conferring titles
- 18(2) → accepting foreign titles
- 18(3) → accepting foreign titles
- 18(4) → accepting present / emolument / office from a foreign State

**Is there an exception?**
- 18(1) → yes: military & academic distinctions
- 18(2) → **NONE — absolute**
- 18(3) → yes: consent of the President
- 18(4) → yes: consent of the President

## 7. Self-test statements (T/F)

1. No citizen of India shall accept any title from any foreign State under any circumstances. → **TRUE** (18(2), absolute)
2. A foreigner holding an office of profit under the State may accept a foreign title with the President's consent. → **TRUE** (18(3))
3. Bharat Ratna is a "title" within the meaning of Art. 18. → **FALSE** (*Balaji Raghavan*, 1996)
4. Bharat Ratna may be used as a suffix to the recipient's name. → **FALSE**
5. Art. 18 prescribes punishment for accepting a foreign title. → **FALSE** (no penalty prescribed; left to Parliament)
6. Art. 18(1) bars the State from conferring military decorations. → **FALSE** (expressly saved)
7. A civil servant needs the President's consent to accept a gift from a foreign State. → **TRUE** (18(4))
