## 1. Why Article 12 matters (the "gatekeeper" article)

- Fundamental Rights are, as a rule, available **against the State** — not against private individuals.
- Article 12 therefore decides **whom you can sue** for a FR violation. It is the *doorway* to Part III.
- Practical effect: it fixes the reach of **Article 32** (SC writ) and, indirectly, **Article 226** (HC writ).
- **Article 36** — in Part IV (DPSP), "State" carries the **same meaning as in Article 12**. So the definition governs both Part III and Part IV.
- The **exceptions** where FRs bind private persons too (horizontal rights): **Art. 15(2), 17, 23, 24** — and now, partly, **Arts. 19 & 21** after *Kaushal Kishor* (2023), see §8.

**Syllabus hook:** GS Paper II — *Indian Constitution: features, significant provisions*. Prelims — Polity & Governance.

## 2. Bare text (learn it verbatim)

> **Article 12.** In this Part, unless the context otherwise requires, **"the State" includes** —
> (a) the **Government and Parliament of India**,
> (b) the **Government and the Legislature of each of the States**, and
> (c) all **local or other authorities**
> — **within the territory of India** *or* **under the control of the Government of India**.

### Anatomy — 4 limbs + 3 loaded phrases

**The four limbs**
1. Government & Parliament of India → Union **executive + legislature** (President, PM, Cabinet, Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha)
2. Government & Legislature of each State → State **executive + legislature** (Governor, CM, Council of Ministers, Vidhan Sabha/Parishad)
3. **All local authorities**
4. **Other authorities**

**Three phrases that carry marks**

🔹 **"includes"** → the definition is **INCLUSIVE, not exhaustive**. Courts can and do add to it. *(Classic prelims trap: "Article 12 exhaustively defines State" → FALSE.)*

🔹 **"unless the context otherwise requires"** → built-in flexibility; the same body may be "State" for one purpose and not another.

🔹 **"within the territory of India OR under the control of the Government of India"** → note the **disjunctive "or"**. An authority **outside** India's territory but under GoI control is still "State". *(Trap: "must be within Indian territory" → FALSE.)*

## 3. Framers' intent — Dr. Ambedkar (Constituent Assembly, 25 Nov 1948, on Draft Art. 7)

Ambedkar explained that the object was to make Fundamental Rights binding on **"every authority which has got either the power to make laws or the power to have discretion vested in it."**

- His stated worry: the State acts through a vast machinery of **local bodies, boards, corporations and licensing authorities**. If FRs bound only the Union and States, the State could **evade** them by routing action through such bodies.
- ➡️ **This is the intellectual seed of the "instrumentality/agency" doctrine.** Use this line to open a Mains answer — it shows you know the *why*, not just the case list.

## 4. Limb 3 — "LOCAL AUTHORITIES"

### 4.1 The statutory anchor

The Constitution **does not define** "local authority." So courts borrow **Section 3(31), General Clauses Act, 1897**:

> "a **Municipal Committee, District Board, Body of Port Commissioners** or **other authority legally entitled to, or entrusted by the Government with, the control or management of a municipal or local fund**."

**Bridge to remember:** **Article 367(1)** makes the General Clauses Act applicable to the interpretation of the Constitution. *(This one line lifts a Mains answer above the coaching-note level.)*

### 4.2 THE case — *Union of India v. R.C. Jain* (1981), SC

**Facts:** Was the **Delhi Development Authority (DDA)** a "local authority" under s. 32(iv), Payment of Bonus Act, 1965?
**Held:** **YES** — DDA is a local authority. The Court laid down **five attributes**.

**Mnemonic: L-A-A-F-S**

**L — separate Legal existence**
Must be a **body corporate**, legally independent — *not a mere government agency/department*.

**A — functions in a defined Area**
…and must **"ordinarily, wholly or partly, directly or indirectly, be elected by the inhabitants"** of that area.
⚠️ Note the word **"ordinarily"** → election is a **strong indicator, NOT a mandatory condition**. That is exactly why the **non-elected DDA still qualified**.

**A — Autonomy**
Must enjoy an **"appreciable measure of autonomy"** — freedom to decide policy for its area. Need **not** be complete autonomy.

**F — power to raise Funds**
By levying **taxes, rates, charges or fees**. May be *in addition to* government grants/borrowings.
**Essential:** **control or management of the fund must vest in the authority itself.**

**S — entrusted by Statute with municipal-type functions**
Governmental functions "usually entrusted to municipal bodies": health & education, water & sewerage, town planning & development, roads, markets, transport, social welfare.

### 4.3 Examples of local authorities
Municipalities · Municipal Corporations · **Panchayats** · District Boards · Improvement Trusts · Port Commissioners · Notified Area / Town Area Committees · **Development Authorities (DDA)** · Mining Boards

## 5. Limb 4 — "OTHER AUTHORITIES" (the heart of the topic)

This is where nearly all the litigation — and all the exam questions — sit. Learn it as **four phases**.

### PHASE 1 — Narrow / restrictive view *(now REJECTED)*

**University of Madras v. Shantha Bai** — **Madras HIGH COURT**, 1 May 1953, **AIR 1954 Mad 67**

- **Facts:** MGM College, Udipi (affiliated to Madras University) was allowed only 10 girl students in Junior Intermediate; the Syndicate directed that **no women be admitted in future without special sanction**. Shantha Bai was refused admission in 1951 and challenged it.
- **Held on Art. 12:** *"These words must be construed **ejusdem generis** with Government or Legislature and so construed can only mean authorities exercising **governmental functions**. They would not include persons natural or juristic who cannot be regarded as instrumentalities of the Government."*
- University of Madras (a body corporate under Madras Act VII of 1923, **aided but not maintained** by the State, existing only to promote education) → **NOT "State."**
- **Second limb:** Art. 29(2) — the specific admissions provision — **omits "sex"** as a ground; applying *generalia specialibus non derogant*, 29(2) prevailed over the general Art. 15(1).
- **Outcome: the University WON.** Shantha Bai lost.

🚨 **This reasoning is DEAD. See Phase 2. Cite Santa Bai only as the starting point of the restrictive era.**

### PHASE 2 — *Ejusdem generis* rejected; statutory bodies brought in

**Ujjam Bai v. State of UP (1962), SC** — Ayyangar J.: "other authorities" **cannot** be read *ejusdem generis* with either the Government/Legislatures or local authorities; the words are of **wide amplitude**, covering every authority created under a statute.

**Rajasthan State Electricity Board v. Mohan Lal (1967), SC** — *the clincher*:
- ***Ejusdem generis* cannot apply because there is NO COMMON GENUS** running through the bodies listed in Article 12.
- "Other authorities" = **all authorities created by the Constitution or by statute on whom powers are conferred by law**.
- ⭐ **It is NOT necessary that the body perform governmental or sovereign functions**; it may even be engaged in **commercial activity**.
- **RSEB held to be "State."**

### PHASE 3 — Beyond statutory bodies: the AGENCY / INSTRUMENTALITY doctrine

**Sukhdev Singh v. Bhagatram (1975), SC** — statutory corporations **ONGC, LIC, IFCI** are "State"; their regulations have the force of law. *(Mathew J.'s concurrence seeded the instrumentality idea.)*

**R.D. Shetty v. International Airport Authority of India (1979), SC** — Bhagwati J. laid down the original **5-point test**. Key insight: **the corporate veil is irrelevant — the State cannot escape FRs by acting through a corporation.**

**Som Prakash Rekhi v. Union of India (1981), SC** — a **government company** (Bharat Petroleum) is "State".

**⭐ Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib Sehravardi (1981), SC** — consolidated Shetty's 5 into the canonical **SIX TESTS**. A **registered society** running REC Srinagar was held to be **State**.

> **The 6 Ajay Hasia tests (verbatim):**
> 1. Whether the **share-capital** of the corporation is held by the Government
> 2. Whether the **financial assistance** of the State meets almost the **entire expenditure** of the corporation
> 3. Whether the corporation enjoys a **State-conferred or State-protected monopoly** status
> 4. Whether there is **deep and pervasive State control**
> 5. Whether the **functions** are of **public importance and closely related to governmental functions**
> 6. Whether a **department of Government has been transferred** to the corporation

**Mnemonic: "S-F-M-C-F-D"** → **S**hare capital · **F**inancial assistance · **M**onopoly · **C**ontrol (deep & pervasive) · **F**unctions of public importance · **D**epartment transferred.

**Grouping (advanced, good for Mains):**
- Tests **1, 2, 4** → measure **government ownership/control**
- Tests **3, 5, 6** → **functional** tests
- ⭐ **Ratio:** *form is irrelevant, **substance of control** decides.* A society, a company, even a private body can be "State".

### PHASE 4 — The FINAL, authoritative position

**⭐⭐ Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. Indian Institute of Chemical Biology (2002) 5 SCC 111**

- **16 April 2002 · SEVEN-judge Constitution Bench · 5:2 majority** (majority by **Ruma Pal J.**)
- **Held: CSIR IS "State"** under Article 12.
- **OVERRULED *Sabhajit Tewary v. Union of India* (1975)** — a 5-judge bench that had held CSIR *not* to be State. *(This is why a 7-judge bench was needed.)*
- **The test:** the body must be **financially, functionally and administratively dominated by, or under the control of, the Government** — and that control must be **"particular to the body" and "deep and pervasive."**
- ⭐ **Mere government patronage, regulation, or financial assistance is NOT enough.** The enquiry is **functional, not formal** — decided case by case on cumulative facts.

**Why it is "the final judgement" (as your handwritten note correctly says):** it is the largest bench, it settled the conflict, and every later case applies its formula.

## 6. IS THE JUDICIARY "STATE" UNDER ARTICLE 12?

The settled dual position:

**❌ NOT "State" — when exercising JUDICIAL functions**
(hearing cases, delivering judgments, passing judicial orders)
**Rationale:** to preserve **judicial independence**; a judicial decision cannot itself be attacked as a FR violation, otherwise litigation would never end.
- **Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar v. State of Maharashtra (1966)** — **9-judge bench**: a **judicial order of the High Court cannot be challenged under Article 32** as violating FRs. The remedy is **appeal, not a writ**.
- **Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra (2002)** — **reaffirmed** that superior courts exercising judicial functions are **not** within "State"/"other authorities", and that **judicial orders cannot be challenged for violating FRs**. *(This is also the case that created the **CURATIVE PETITION** — a high-value add-on fact.)*

**✅ IS "State" — when exercising ADMINISTRATIVE / RULE-MAKING functions**
(appointments to court staff, service matters, framing rules under Arts. 145/227, administrative decisions)
Here the court acts like any other authority and **is** bound by Part III.

**Mnemonic:** **"Judging → NOT State. Managing/Rule-making → State."**

**⚡ Advanced nuance (Mains/interview level, beyond prelims):**
**A.R. Antulay v. R.S. Nayak (1988), 7-judge bench** sits somewhat uneasily with *Mirajkar*. There the SC held that **its own earlier direction** transferring a trial was **without jurisdiction and violated Arts. 14 & 21**, and recalled it. So the SC has, in substance, accepted that a **judicial order can infringe FRs** — even if the *remedy* is recall/review/curative rather than an Art. 32 writ. Coaching material usually flattens this. Don't cite Antulay as simply "rule-making = State"; cite it as the **exception that shows judicial orders are not wholly immune.**

## 7. SCORECARD — Who is "State", who is not

### ✅ HELD TO BE "STATE"
- **Rajasthan State Electricity Board** — *RSEB v. Mohan Lal* (1967)
- **ONGC, LIC, IFCI** — *Sukhdev Singh v. Bhagatram* (1975)
- **International Airport Authority** — *R.D. Shetty* (1979)
- **Society running REC Srinagar** — *Ajay Hasia* (1981)
- **Bharat Petroleum (govt. company)** — *Som Prakash Rekhi* (1981)
- **Delhi Development Authority** *(as a local authority)* — *R.C. Jain* (1981)
- **CSIR** — *Pradeep Kumar Biswas* (2002) ⭐
- **SAIL, other PSUs meeting the tests**
- Municipalities, Panchayats, District Boards, Improvement Trusts, Port Commissioners

### ❌ HELD NOT TO BE "STATE"
- **University of Madras** — *Santa Bai* (1954, Mad HC) *— reasoning since rejected*
- **CSIR** — *Sabhajit Tewary* (1975) ⚠️ **OVERRULED in 2002**
- **NCERT** — *Chander Mohan Khanna v. NCERT* (1991)
- **BCCI** — *Zee Telefilms v. Union of India* (2005)
- **Judiciary**, when performing **judicial** functions

### The two "not State" cases your pages highlight — details

**Chander Mohan Khanna v. NCERT (1991), SC** — 17 Sept 1991
- Employee's termination challenged; NCERT objected that it is not amenable to writ jurisdiction.
- **Held: NCERT is NOT "State".** It is **"largely an autonomous body"**; its activities are **not wholly related to governmental functions**; government control is **confined only to proper utilisation of the grant**; its funds include grants *plus* contributions from other sources *plus* income from its own assets, which it is free to apply towards its own objectives.
- ⭐ **The quotable line:** *"Article 12 should not be stretched so as to bring in every autonomous body which has some nexus with the Government within the sweep of the expression 'State'. A wide enlargement of the meaning must be tempered by a wise limitation."*

**Zee Telefilms Ltd. v. Union of India (2005) 4 SCC 649** — 5-judge bench, **3:2**
- **Held: BCCI is NOT "State"** under Art. 12 → **no Art. 32 remedy**.
- **Reasons:** not created by any statute; **no share capital held by government**; **no substantial financial assistance** from government; its monopoly over cricket administration is **neither conferred nor protected by the State** (it is *de facto*, not *de jure*); no deep and pervasive control.
- ⭐ **BUT — the crucial second half your book omits:** because BCCI discharges **public functions** (selecting the national team, regulating the sport), an aggrieved person **CAN move the High Court under Article 226**.
- **Therefore: Art. 226 is WIDER than Art. 32.** Art. 32 lies only against "State"; **Art. 226 lies additionally against a private body performing a public duty** — *Andi Mukta Sadguru Trust v. V.R. Rudani* (1989).
- **Current-affairs link:** *BCCI v. Cricket Association of Bihar* (2015) → SC affirmed BCCI's public functions, appointed the **Justice R.M. Lodha Committee**; reforms later folded into BCCI's constitution.

## 8. PRIVATE BODIES, PUBLIC FUNCTIONS & HORIZONTAL RIGHTS

**The general rule:** private bodies are **not automatically** "State".

**Three routes by which a private entity can still be reached:**
1. **Instrumentality/agency** → it *is* State (Ajay Hasia / Pradeep Kumar Biswas tests)
2. **Deep and pervasive State control** over functioning, finance and policy
3. **Public function test** → not State, but still amenable to **Art. 226** writ *(Zee Telefilms; Andi Mukta)*

**⭐ THE BIG RECENT SHIFT — *Kaushal Kishor v. State of UP* (3 Jan 2023), 5-judge Constitution Bench, 4:1**
- Held that **Fundamental Rights under Articles 19 and 21 can be enforced even against PRIVATE / NON-STATE ACTORS.**
- i.e. Arts. 19 and 21 are, to that extent, **HORIZONTAL**.
- Also held: the grounds in **Art. 19(2) are exhaustive** — no additional restrictions can be read in.
- **Significance for this topic:** it **dilutes Article 12's gatekeeping role**. The old binary ("State → FRs apply; private → they don't") is breaking down.
- 🔥 **This is the single best value-addition for a Mains answer or interview on Article 12.** Very few aspirants link it.

**Pre-existing horizontal FRs (available against private persons too):**
- **Art. 15(2)** — no denial of access to shops, hotels, public restaurants, wells, tanks, roads
- **Art. 17** — abolition of untouchability
- **Art. 23** — traffic in human beings, *begar*, forced labour
- **Art. 24** — no child below 14 in factories/mines/hazardous work

*(Note: Arts. 17, 23, 24 are exactly the ones in the table on the top of your first page — that table is the horizontal-rights set. Good linkage to make.)*

## 9. LINKAGES — the articles that sit around Art. 12

**Article 13** *(where your Sarrthi page goes next)*
- **13(1):** pre-Constitution laws inconsistent with Part III are **void to the extent of inconsistency**
- **13(2):** the **State shall not make** any law taking away/abridging FRs
- **⭐ 13(3)(a):** **"law" includes** any *ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage* having the force of law
  → **This is the bridge:** *Mohammad Yasin* struck down a **Town Area Committee's bye-laws** — possible only because the Committee is "State" under Art. 12 **and** its bye-laws are "law" under Art. 13(3)(a).
- **13(4):** a constitutional amendment under Art. 368 is **not** "law" *(inserted by 24th Amendment; read with **Kesavananda Bharati, 1973** — amendments are still testable on the **basic structure** doctrine)*
- **Doctrines under Art. 13:** Severability · Eclipse · Waiver *(FRs cannot be waived — Behram Khurshid Pesikaka; Basheshar Nath v. CIT)*

**Article 32** — writ against "State" only (a FR itself; Ambedkar's *"heart and soul of the Constitution"*)
**Article 226** — **wider**: any legal right + against private bodies performing public duties
**Article 36** — "State" in Part IV = same meaning as Art. 12
**Article 367(1)** — General Clauses Act applies to constitutional interpretation *(the bridge for "local authority")*

## 10. 🚨 CORRECTIONS TO YOUR SOURCE PAGES

**❌ ERROR 1 (printed book, p.33): "In *Mohammad Yasin vs Town Area Committee*, the Supreme Court gave four criteria for an entity to be classified as a 'local authority' under Article 12."**

**WRONG.** *Mohammad Yasin v. Town Area Committee, Jalalabad* (AIR 1952 SC 115, decided 27 Feb 1952) is an **Article 19(1)(g) / Article 32** case. It lays down **no** test for "local authority" anywhere in the judgment.
- **Its actual ratio:** a Town Area Committee's bye-laws levying a licence fee (one anna per rupee) on wholesale fruit/vegetable dealers **irrespective of use of Committee property** were **ultra vires** the U.P. Municipalities Act; *"a licence fee on a business… operates as a restriction on his right to carry on his business"* under Art. 19(1)(g), not saved by 19(6). Art. 265 also invoked.
- **The correct case for the definition of "local authority" is *Union of India v. R.C. Jain* (1981) — the LAAFS five attributes** (your handwritten ForumIAS note is right).
- **Where the book's "four criteria" actually come from:** they are a garbled import from the **"other authorities"** line — (a),(b),(c) from *Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal* (1967), and (d) "substantially financed by government" from the *Ajay Hasia* / *Pradeep Kumar Biswas* instrumentality tests. The book has **merged the two branches of Article 12.**

**❌ ERROR 2 (printed book, p.33): *University of Madras v. Santa Bai* (1954) presented as current law.**

Its ***ejusdem generis*** reasoning was **expressly rejected** by the Supreme Court in **Ujjam Bai (1962)** and buried in **Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal (1967)** ("no common genus"). It is a **High Court** decision and is **no longer good law.**
- Also: the book's phrase *"unless it is **maintained by the State**"* is **not part of the Article 12 test at all** — that is the language of **Article 29(2)** ("maintained by the State or receiving aid out of State funds"), which the HC used in the *separate* discrimination limb. The book has welded a bit of Art. 29(2) onto the Art. 12 holding.
- **Irony worth remembering:** under *Santa Bai* a university was not State; under *Ajay Hasia* / *Pradeep Kumar Biswas*, universities, societies and even private bodies **routinely are**, because control and funding matter — not "sovereign functions."

**❌ ERROR 3 (Sarrthi IAS, p.6): "In *Rati Lal v. State of Bombay* — the judiciary is not state for the purpose of Article 12."**

**Unsupported.** *Ratilal Panachand Gandhi v. State of Bombay* (1954) is a case on the **Bombay Public Trusts Act, 1950** and **Articles 25 & 26** (freedom of religion, rights of religious denominations). The judgment **does not discuss Article 12 or the judiciary's status at all.** This citation is copied through secondary material.
- **Use instead:** **Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar (1966)** and **Rupa Ashok Hurra (2002)**.

**⚠️ MINOR (Sarrthi):** the case is **Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar**, not "N.S. Mirajkar" — write the full name in Mains. And see §6 for why **A.R. Antulay** should not be cited as a simple "rule-making = State" authority.

Shetty → Ajay Hasia 6 parameters → Pradeep Kumar Biswas as final, the judiciary dual position, and NCERT ≠ State / BCCI ≠ State. Your own notes are **more accurate than the printed book.**

## 11. PRELIMS RAPID-FIRE (one-liners & traps)

**TRUE / high-yield**
- Art. 12's definition is **inclusive**, not exhaustive
- "State" in Art. 12 governs **Part III AND Part IV** (via Art. 36)
- A body **outside Indian territory** but under GoI control **is** "State"
- **Election by inhabitants is not mandatory** for local-authority status ("ordinarily")
- Under *Rajasthan SEB* (1967), a body **need not perform sovereign functions** to be "State"; it may be **commercial**
- **CSIR is State** (2002, 7-judge, overruling 1975)
- **Art. 226 is wider than Art. 32** — it reaches private bodies doing public duties
- **Arts. 19 & 21 are now partly horizontal** (*Kaushal Kishor*, 2023)

**FALSE — the traps**
- ❌ "'Other authorities' is interpreted ***ejusdem generis***" → **rejected** in 1962/1967
- ❌ "Article 12 exhaustively defines the State"
- ❌ "All PSUs / government companies are automatically State" → **only if the tests are satisfied**
- ❌ "The judiciary is never State" → it **is**, in administrative/rule-making functions
- ❌ "BCCI is State" → **not** under Art. 12 (but writ lies under Art. 226)
- ❌ "NCERT is State"
- ❌ "Mohammad Yasin laid down the local-authority test"
- ❌ "Mere government financial assistance makes a body State"

## 12. MAINS ANGLE (GS-II)

**Likely framings**
1. *"The expression 'other authorities' in Article 12 has been judicially expanded far beyond the framers' contemplation. Examine."*
2. *"Article 12 is the gatekeeper of Part III. In an era of privatisation and PPP, is that gate too narrow?" Critically comment.*
3. *"Should the judiciary be brought within the definition of 'State' under Article 12?" Discuss.*

**Skeleton for Q1/Q2**
- **Intro:** Ambedkar's intent — FRs must bind *"every authority which has… the power to have discretion vested in it"* — quote it.
- **Body — the arc:** restrictive (*Santa Bai*, *ejusdem generis*) → rejection (*Ujjam Bai*, *Rajasthan SEB*) → instrumentality (*Shetty*, *Ajay Hasia*) → consolidation & **a deliberate brake** (*Pradeep Kumar Biswas*; *NCERT* — *"a wide enlargement must be tempered by a wise limitation"*).
- **The contemporary problem:** liberalisation, disinvestment, PPP, outsourced public services (private hospitals, private schools, toll roads, Aadhaar-linked private vendors, platform economy) → State functions migrate to private hands, **outside** Art. 12.
- **The judicial answer so far:** the **public function test** under Art. 226 (*Zee Telefilms*, *Andi Mukta*) and now **horizontality** under *Kaushal Kishor* (2023).
- **Balance:** over-expansion would burden genuinely autonomous institutions and chill private enterprise (the *NCERT* caution); under-inclusion lets the State evade FRs by outsourcing.
- **Conclusion:** the trend is from an **institutional/formal** test to a **functional** one — *what is being done*, not *who is doing it*. Suggest a **function-based** reading of Art. 12 consistent with Ambedkar's purpose.

**Value-addition arsenal**
- Ambedkar's CAD quote (25 Nov 1948)
- *NCERT*: "wise limitation" line
- *Pradeep Kumar Biswas*: "financially, functionally, administratively dominated"
- *Kaushal Kishor* (2023): horizontality
- Comparative: US **"state action" doctrine**; **South Africa** — Art. 8(2) of its Constitution expressly binds private persons to the Bill of Rights *(a strong comparative line)*
- **2nd ARC**, and Art. 226 public-function jurisprudence, on accountability of outsourced public services

**Q1.** Consider the following statements regarding Article 12:
1. The definition of "State" given in Article 12 is exhaustive.
2. An authority located outside the territory of India cannot be "State" even if it is under the control of the Government of India.
3. "State" in Article 12 has the same meaning for the Directive Principles.
Which are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 (b) 3 only (c) 2 and 3 (d) 1, 2 and 3
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 3 only.** 1 is wrong — "includes" makes it inclusive. 2 is wrong — the "or" is disjunctive. 3 is right — **Article 36**.

**Q2.** In which case did the Supreme Court hold that "other authorities" cannot be read *ejusdem generis* with the preceding expressions in Article 12?
(a) University of Madras v. Santa Bai (b) Rajasthan State Electricity Board v. Mohan Lal (c) Mohammad Yasin v. Town Area Committee (d) Union of India v. R.C. Jain

**(b).** Because there is **no common genus** among the bodies listed. Ujjam Bai (1962) had said so earlier; RSEB (1967) settled it. (a) held the **opposite** and is no longer good law.

(a) 1 and 2 (b) 2 and 3 (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 3 and 4
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 and 3.** CSIR **is** State (*Pradeep Kumar Biswas*, 2002 — overruling *Sabhajit Tewary*, 1975 — a favourite trap). RSEB **is** State (1967).
</details>

**Q4.** Regarding the judiciary and Article 12, which is correct?
(a) The judiciary is "State" for all purposes
(b) The judiciary is never "State"
(c) The judiciary is "State" when exercising administrative and rule-making functions, but not when exercising judicial functions
(d) Only subordinate courts are "State"
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c).** *Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar* (1966), reaffirmed in *Rupa Ashok Hurra* (2002).
</details>

**Q5.** Consider:
1. BCCI is "State" under Article 12 and hence a writ under Article 32 lies against it.
2. A private body performing a public duty may be subject to a writ under Article 226.
Which is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 only (c) Both (d) Neither
<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only.** *Zee Telefilms* (2005): BCCI is **not** State → no Art. 32; but Art. 226 lies for its **public functions**. Art. 226 is wider than Art. 32.
</details>
