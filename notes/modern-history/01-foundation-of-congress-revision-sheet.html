Regional Integration — the thread

Local reform bodies → regional political outfits → one national platform (INC).

The Congress was not a sudden event. From the early 19th century, reform bodies, newspapers & journals awakened opinion; similar demands pulled scattered local associations into regional ones, which finally integrated into a national organisation. — the accepted view (Bipan Chandra).

Pre-Congress Political Associations

Match name ↔ founder ↔ year ↔ city — a favourite Prelims trap.

🅐 Bengal (Calcutta)

1836

Bangabhasha Prakashika Sabha

Associates of Raja Rammohan Roy. One of the earliest political associations.

1838

Landholders' Society

Dwarkanath Tagore · Raja Radhakant Deb. First organised political effort; guarded zamindar interests.

1843

Bengal British India Society

On advice of George Thompson. Publish info on the real condition of the people.

1851

British Indian Association

Merger of the two above. Zamindar-dominated; conservative.

🔎
Don't confuse the three "British India" bodies:  British India Society = 1839, London, William Adam · Bengal British India Society = 1843, Calcutta · British Indian Association = 1851, the merger.

🅑 Bombay · 🅒 Madras · 🅓 Poona · 🅔 England

1852

Bombay Association

Jagannath Shankar Seth · Dadabhai Naoroji. Petitioned on the Charter Act 1853.

1885

Bombay Presidency Association

Pherozeshah Mehta · K.T. Telang · Badruddin Tyabji — the “Bombay Trio”.

1852

Madras Native Association

Gazulu Lakshminarasu Chetty. Raised ryotwari peasant grievances; short-lived.

1884

Madras Mahajan Sabha

Subramania Iyer · Anandacharlu · Viraraghavachariar.

1870

Poona Sarvajanik Sabha

M.G. Ranade & others. 1875 petition to the House of Commons for direct representation — signed by 23,000+.

1866

East India Association

Dadabhai Naoroji, in London. Swayed British public opinion in India's favour.

1876

Indian (National) Association

S.N. Banerjee & Anand Mohan Bose. First avowedly nationalist body; merged into INC (1886).

1878

National Mohammedan Association

Syed Amir Ali.

🧠
Bombay Trio = “M-T-T” → Mehta · Telang · Tyabji.  •  Common demands (fed the Charter Act 1853 petitions): popular legislation · separation of executive & judiciary · cut official salaries · abolish salt & stamp duty.

Why was the INC born? — four theories

The classic Mains debate: British “safety valve” or Indian awakening?

S

Safety-Valve Theory — attributed to A.O. Hume's design

INC set up as a “safety valve” to release mass discontent and pre-empt a violent revolt.

L

Lightning-Conductor Theory — G.K. Gokhale

Nationalists deliberately kept Hume (a white ex-civil servant) in front as a shield, so the British would not crush the infant Congress — Congress channelled nationalist forces safely.

C

Conspiracy Theory — R.P. Dutt, India Today (1940)

Extends the safety-valve idea: INC born of a conspiracy to abort a popular uprising; bourgeois leaders were complacent partners.

✓

Conscious-Efforts view — accepted — Bipan Chandra

INC = the culmination of decades of conscious Indian effort; early leaders were patriots of high character, not government stooges.

🧠
Recall order S · L · C · C — and the last C (Conscious efforts) is the one to write as your conclusion.

The constitutional reply — Councils Acts

What the Raj conceded, and how little it meant.

Indian Councils Act, 1861

Portfolio + Decentralisation
  • Portfolio system — begun by Canning (1858–59), recognised by this Act (Military, Revenue, Finance, Home, Law; Public Works added 1874).
  • Beginning of decentralisation — legislative powers restored to Bombay & Madras; later councils for NW Provinces, Punjab, Burma.
  • Viceroy may issue ordinances (valid 6 months).
  • Additional (non-official) members for law-making — nominated, 2-yr term. First Indians: Raja of Banaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao.
  • Budget: ✗ cannot discuss · Questions: ✗ cannot ask.

Indian Councils Act, 1892

Representation + Budget discussion
  • Executive Council — no change.
  • Additional members raised to 10–16 in the central council (≈ 6 official + 10 non-official; 5 Viceroy-named + 1 each from Bengal, Bombay, Madras, NW Provinces & Bengal Chamber of Commerce).
  • Introduced the principle of representation → first indirect “election” (word avoided; called “nomination on recommendation”).
  • Budget: ✓ can discuss (✗ cannot vote).
  • Questions: ✓ can ask (✗ no supplementary).
