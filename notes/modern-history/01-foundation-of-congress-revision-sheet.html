Rise of Nationalism and the Foundation of the Congress

Pre-Congress associations, the theories of the INC's birth, the Moderate and Extremist phases, and the constitutional reply of 1861 and 1892.

GS-I Mains Prelims high-yield 1836 to 1919 Adarsh | UPSC CSE
1

Regional Integration - the thread

Local reform bodies, then regional political outfits, then one national platform (INC).

The accepted view - Bipan Chandra The Congress was not a sudden event. From the early 19th century, reform bodies, newspapers and journals awakened opinion; similar demands pulled scattered local associations into regional ones, which finally integrated into a national organisation.
2

Pre-Congress Political Associations

Match name, founder, year and city - a favourite Prelims trap.

A. Bengal (Calcutta)

1836

Bangabhasha Prakashika Sabha

Associates of Raja Rammohan Roy. One of the earliest political associations.

1838

Landholders' Society

Dwarkanath Tagore and Raja Radhakant Deb. First organised political effort; guarded zamindar interests.

1843

Bengal British India Society

On the advice of George Thompson. Publish info on the real condition of the people.

1851

British Indian Association

Merger of the two above. Zamindar-dominated; conservative.

Do not confuse the three "British India" bodies British India Society = 1839, London, William Adam  |  Bengal British India Society = 1843, Calcutta  |  British Indian Association = 1851, the merger.

B. Bombay, Madras, Poona and England

1852

Bombay Association

Jagannath Shankar Seth and Dadabhai Naoroji. Petitioned on the Charter Act 1853.

1885

Bombay Presidency Association

Pherozeshah Mehta, K.T. Telang, Badruddin Tyabji - the "Bombay Trio".

1852

Madras Native Association

Gazulu Lakshminarasu Chetty. Raised ryotwari peasant grievances; short-lived.

1884

Madras Mahajan Sabha

Subramania Iyer, Anandacharlu, Viraraghavachariar.

1870

Poona Sarvajanik Sabha

M.G. Ranade and others. 1875 petition to the House of Commons for direct representation - signed by 23,000+.

1866

East India Association

Dadabhai Naoroji, in London. Swayed British public opinion in India's favour.

1876

Indian (National) Association

S.N. Banerjee and Anand Mohan Bose. First avowedly nationalist body; merged into INC (1886).

1878

National Mohammedan Association

Syed Amir Ali.

Memory hook and common demands Bombay Trio = "M-T-T" (Mehta, Telang, Tyabji).   Common demands that fed the Charter Act 1853 petitions: popular legislation; separation of executive and judiciary; cut official salaries; abolish salt and stamp duty.

Why was the INC born? Four theories

The classic Mains debate: a British "safety valve" or an Indian awakening?

S

Safety-Valve Theory - attributed to A.O. Hume's design

The INC was set up as a "safety valve" to release mass discontent and pre-empt a violent revolt.

L

Lightning-Conductor Theory - G.K. Gokhale

Nationalists deliberately kept Hume (a white ex-civil servant) in front as a shield, so the British would not crush the infant Congress. The Congress channelled nationalist forces safely.

C

Conspiracy Theory - R.P. Dutt, in India Today (1940)

Extends the safety-valve idea: the INC was born of a conspiracy to abort a popular uprising; the bourgeois leaders were complacent partners.

4

Conscious-Efforts view - the accepted one - Bipan Chandra

The INC was the culmination of decades of conscious Indian effort; early leaders were patriots of high character, not government stooges.

Memory hook Recall the order S, L, C, then the 4th (Conscious efforts) - and write that last one as your conclusion.

The constitutional reply - the Councils Acts

What the Raj conceded, and how little it meant.

Indian Councils Act, 1861

Portfolio + Decentralisation
  • Portfolio system - begun by Canning (1858-59), recognised by this Act (Military, Revenue, Finance, Home, Law; Public Works added 1874).
  • Beginning of decentralisation - legislative powers restored to Bombay and Madras; later councils for NW Provinces, Punjab and Burma.
  • The Viceroy may issue ordinances (valid 6 months).
  • Additional (non-official) members added for law-making - nominated, 2-year term. First Indians: Raja of Banaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao.
  • Budget: cannot discuss. Questions: cannot ask.

Indian Councils Act, 1892

Representation + Budget discussion
  • Executive Council - no change.
  • Additional members raised to 10 to 16 in the central council (about 6 official and 10 non-official; 5 Viceroy-named and 1 each from Bengal, Bombay, Madras, NW Provinces and the Bengal Chamber of Commerce).
  • Introduced the principle of representation - the first indirect "election" (the word "election" was avoided; called "nomination on recommendation").
  • Budget: can discuss (but cannot vote).
  • Questions: can ask (but no supplementary ones).
