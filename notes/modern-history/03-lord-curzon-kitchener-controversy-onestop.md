# 1. Lord Curzon — The Man & The Tenure

- **Full title:** **George Nathaniel Curzon**, Viceroy & Governor-General of India
  **1899–1905** (appointed under a Conservative government; PM Salisbury, later Balfour).
- 🎯 **One-line identity:** the most **energetic, imperialist, and controversial** Viceroy —
  a brilliant administrator convinced of Britain's "**civilising mission**", contemptuous of
  Indian nationalism, and a compulsive **efficiency-and-centralisation** reformer.
- His famous view: he saw the **Congress as "tottering to its fall"** and wanted to "**assist
  it to a peaceful demise**" — yet his own **Partition of Bengal (1905)** did more to
  galvanise nationalism than anything the Moderates had achieved. ✍️ *(favourite irony line
  for Mains.)*
- ⚠️ Tenure had **two innings:** 1899–1904 (first term), then a short second term 1904–1905
  cut off by the Kitchener dispute.

# 2. ⭐ THE ANCHOR — Kitchener–Curzon Controversy (1904–05)

> This is the exact content of the handwritten page — expanded and exam-verified.

## 2.1 The two men
- **Lord Curzon** → the **Governor-General / Viceroy** (the supreme *civil* authority).
- **Lord Kitchener** → **Commander-in-Chief (C-in-C)** of the Indian Army from **1902**
  (a war hero, victor of Omdurman & the Boer War, immense prestige in Britain).

## 2.2 The system in dispute — "dual control" of the army
Before the clash, control over the Indian Army was **split between two men**:

- **The Commander-in-Chief (Kitchener):**
  → in charge of **military command & operations** (the fighting side).

- **The Military Member of the Viceroy's Executive Council:**
  → in charge of the **administrative side** — **supplies, transport, ordnance, finance,
     stores** — and, crucially, **advised the Viceroy** on army matters.
  → 🎯 The Military Member was the **"eyes and ears of the Governor-General in the Army"**
     (*this is the exact phrase in the handwritten note*) — i.e., the **civilian check** that
     let the Viceroy supervise the C-in-C rather than take the Army's word blindly.

🧠 **Mnemonic — "C-in-C Commands, Military Member Manages."**

## 2.3 What Kitchener wanted
- Kitchener resented sharing power. He argued **dual control was inefficient and dangerous**
  in wartime and demanded the **abolition of the office of the Military Member**, so that
  **all military authority — command *and* administration — is concentrated in the C-in-C**
  (himself).

## 2.4 What Curzon wanted
- Curzon **strongly opposed** this. He defended the Military Member as an essential
  **safeguard of civilian supremacy** — a check that keeps the Army answerable to the
  Viceroy-in-Council, not a law unto itself.
- ✍️ **The real issue:** *civil authority vs military authority* — should the Army be
  supervised by the civil government, or run by the soldiers alone?

## 2.5 How it was decided — London backs the soldier
- The quarrel was **referred to London** (the **Secretary of State for India, St John
  Brodrick**).
- **Jan 1905:** Brodrick proposed an inquiry; **May 1905:** the British **Cabinet approved
  Brodrick's report abolishing the Military Member**, redistributing its duties to the
  **C-in-C** and to a new, weaker post — the **"Military Supply Member."**
- ⚠️ The **British Government (PM Arthur Balfour) sided with Kitchener over Curzon** — partly
  because of Kitchener's popularity and partly because London was tired of Curzon's
  high-handedness.

## 2.6 The resignation — Nov 1905
- The final break came over the **appointment / powers of the new Military Supply Member**
  (Curzon's nominee and his idea of the post were overruled).
- Feeling **undermined and unsupported**, **Curzon submitted his resignation in August 1905**;
  it was accepted, and he **left India in November 1905** *(the "Nov. 1905" circled in the
  handwritten note)*.
- He was succeeded by **Lord Minto II (1905–1910)**.

## 2.7 Why it matters (significance)
- 🎯 A rare case where **the soldier (C-in-C) won and the Viceroy lost** — showing how much
  prestige Kitchener commanded and how isolated Curzon had become even in London.
- ✍️ Constitutionally it weakened the **principle of civilian control over the military** in
  India for the moment — though the Military Member post was **later effectively restored**
  in a modified form.
- It ended the viceroyalty of India's most dynamic and most divisive administrator — and he
  departed just as his **Partition of Bengal** was detonating the **Swadeshi movement**.

# 3. Curzon's Administrative Record (the reforms Prelims loves) ⚠️

> Curzon = a "**Commission Raj**": he appointed a commission for almost every field, then
> legislated. High-frequency Prelims zone — learn the Act/Commission + year.

## 3.1 Education
- **Universities Commission (1902)** under **Thomas Raleigh** →
- **Indian Universities Act, 1904** — tightened **government control over universities**
  (fewer, nominated Senate members; Government veto on affiliation; stress on teaching &
  research over mere examining). Nationalists condemned it as an attempt to **curb the spread
  of higher education** and political ideas — Gokhale called it "**retrograde**."

## 3.2 Police
- **Police Commission (1902)** under **Sir Andrew Fraser** → recommended reform of a corrupt,
  inefficient force → led to the creation of the **Criminal Investigation Department (CID)** at
  provincial level and the **Central Intelligence Department** at the centre. ⚠️

## 3.3 Economy / Famine / Irrigation / Agriculture
- **Famine Commission (1901)** under **Sir Anthony (Antony) MacDonnell** (after the great
  1899–1900 famine).
- **Irrigation Commission (1901)** under **Sir Colin Scott-Moncrieff**.
- Created a separate **Department of Commerce & Industry**.
- **Agriculture:** set up the **Imperial (Agricultural Research) Institute at Pusa (1905)** and
  appointed an **Inspector-General of Agriculture**; established agricultural banks / cooperative
  credit (**Cooperative Credit Societies Act, 1904**).
- **Currency:** carried forward the move to the **gold(-exchange) standard** (**Indian Coinage
  & Paper Currency Act, 1899**).
- **Railways:** appointed the **Robertson Commission** → reorganised railway management under a
  **Railway Board (1905)**.

## 3.4 Land / Local Government
- **Calcutta Corporation Act, 1899** — reduced the number of elected Indian members, giving
  officials a majority (nationalists saw it as throttling self-government).
- **Punjab Land Alienation Act, 1900** — restricted transfer of land from agriculturists to
  moneylenders (to check peasant indebtedness).

## 3.5 Culture & Heritage 🎯 (his one universally-praised legacy)
- **Ancient Monuments Preservation Act, 1904** — a landmark law to protect India's monuments.
- **Revitalised the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)** — appointed **John Marshall** as
  Director-General; funded restoration (Taj Mahal, etc.). ✍️ *"Even nationalists concede
  Curzon's genuine service to Indian archaeology and heritage."*

## 3.6 Frontier, Foreign & Defence
- Created the **North-West Frontier Province (NWFP) in 1901** — carved out of Punjab, placed
  under a Chief Commissioner directly under the Centre, to manage the volatile tribal frontier.
- **Younghusband Mission to Tibet (1903–04)** — a military expedition to check feared Russian
  influence (the "**Great Game**"); forced a trade convention on Lhasa.
- Reorganised the frontier's defence and railways.

🧠 **Mnemonic for Curzon's Acts — "PACU-C":** **P**olice(CID)/**P**usa, **A**ncient
Monuments 1904, **C**alcutta Corporation 1899 & **C**oinage 1899, **U**niversities Act 1904,
plus **C**ommerce & Industry dept and **N**WFP 1901.

# 4. Partition of Bengal (1905) — His Most Fateful Act ⚠️✍️

> The handwritten page stops at his departure, but Curzon's viceroyalty is inseparable from
> the Partition — the bridge to the next phase of nationalism. (Deep-dive belongs to the
> Swadeshi note; here is the exam-minimum.)

- **Announced:** July 1905 · **Effective:** **16 October 1905** (during Curzon's last months).
- **Stated (official) reason:** **administrative convenience** — Bengal was too large
  (~78 million people) to govern.
- **Real (political) motive — "Divide et Impera":** to **break the unity of Bengali-speaking
  people** and drive a **Hindu–Muslim wedge** — the new province of **Eastern Bengal & Assam**
  had a **Muslim majority**, while the west became Hindu-majority. It aimed to weaken the
  nerve-centre of Indian nationalism (Bengal).
- **Reaction:** triggered the **Anti-Partition & Swadeshi Movement (1905)** — *Boycott,
  Swadeshi, National Education, Passive Resistance* — the launch pad of the Extremist phase.
- **Partition annulled in 1911** (Delhi Durbar; capital also shifted Calcutta → Delhi).
- 🎯 **Exam hook:** the Partition, not the Kitchener quarrel, is why Curzon is remembered as
  the Viceroy who *unintentionally* fathered mass nationalism.

# 5. Assessment of Curzon (for Mains) ✍️

- **The efficient imperialist:** unmatched administrative energy; genuine reforms in police,
  education control, agriculture, currency, railways, and heritage.
- **The arrogant centraliser:** contempt for Indians and Indian opinion; his measures
  (Universities Act, Calcutta Corporation Act, Official Secrets Act tightening) were read as
  attempts to **muzzle nationalism and self-government**.
- **The self-inflicted fall:** the **Kitchener controversy** showed a Viceroy so rigid he
  lost even his own government's confidence.
- **The historical irony (model conclusion line):** *"Curzon set out to pronounce the
  Congress's funeral oration; instead, through the Partition of Bengal, he breathed new,
  militant life into it."*

# 7. ⏱️ 60-Second Last-Minute Revision Box

- **Curzon = Viceroy 1899–1905**, most imperialist/energetic; wanted Congress to "die."
- **Kitchener–Curzon controversy:** C-in-C (Kitchener, from 1902) wanted the **Military
  Member** post **abolished** → all army control to himself. Curzon defended the Military
  Member = **"eyes & ears of the Viceroy in the Army"** (civilian check).
- London (**Secy of State Brodrick**, PM **Balfour**) **backed Kitchener** → Military Member
  abolished, replaced by weak **Military Supply Member**.
- **Curzon resigned Aug 1905 → left India Nov 1905** → succeeded by **Lord Minto II**.
- **Key Acts:** Universities Act **1904**, Ancient Monuments Act **1904**, Calcutta Corp Act
  **1899**, Coinage Act **1899**, **NWFP 1901**, **CID** (Police Commission 1902), Pusa
  Institute **1905**, Railway Board **1905**.
- **Partition of Bengal:** announced **July 1905**, effective **16 Oct 1905** → **Swadeshi
  Movement**; **annulled 1911**.
