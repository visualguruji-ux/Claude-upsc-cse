Erosion vs Weathering - the classic trap

UPSC repeatedly tests whether you can separate these two. Keep the pairing sharp:

EROSION

Static process - no movement
Dynamic process - involves movement

In-situ (in place)
Ex-situ (material carried away)

One-line link

Weathering prepares the loose material (regolith); erosion removes and transports it. Together with mass movement they are the degradational (destructional) geomorphic processes.

Agents of erosion & their zones

Running water (rivers)Most important agent overall; dominant in humid regions - valleys, gorges, waterfalls, badlands/ravines.
Glaciers (moving ice)High latitudes & high mountains - U-shaped valleys, cirques, horns, aretes.
Wind (aeolian)Hot deserts & arid/semi-arid lands - mushroom rocks, yardangs, deflation hollows.
Sea wavesCoastlines - sea cliffs, sea caves, arches, stacks, wave-cut platforms.
GroundwaterLimestone (karst) regions - sinkholes, caves, caverns (chemical erosion / solution).

Processes (how an agent actually erodes)

  • Hydraulic action - sheer force of moving water/waves loosening & dislodging rock.
  • Abrasion / Corrasion - the load (sand, pebbles) carried by the agent grinds the bed & banks.
  • Attrition - transported fragments collide and wear each other down, getting smaller & rounder.
  • Corrosion / Solution - chemical dissolving of soluble rocks (e.g. limestone by carbonic acid).
  • Deflation - wind lifts & blows away loose fine particles (specific to aeolian erosion).

Erosion in the Indian context (answer fodder)

  • Gully & ravine erosion - the Chambal ravines (MP/Rajasthan/UP badlands) - classic example of running-water erosion on soft alluvium.
  • Sheet & rill erosion - loss of topsoil on deforested slopes; major cause of land degradation.
  • Coastal erosion - retreating shorelines along parts of Kerala, Odisha, Tamil Nadu.
  • Wind erosion - Thar desert (Rajasthan) - shifting sand, deflation.
  • Data edge (your strength): India loses large quantities of topsoil annually to erosion - link to soil health, food security & desertification (UNCCD) in Mains.
Part 4 - PYQ radar (UPSC + State PSC)

How this is asked

Prelims (UPSC): matching agents of erosion with the landforms they create (e.g. cirque → glacier, yardang → wind, stack → sea waves) is a recurring format.
Prelims trap: statements that swap "weathering" and "erosion" - remember weathering = static/in-situ, erosion = dynamic/ex-situ.
Mains (GS-I): "Distinguish between weathering and erosion" / "Discuss the role of running water as an agent of erosion in shaping landforms."
State PSC (UPPSC/BPSC/MPPSC): direct one-liners - "Which is a dynamic exogenetic process?" (Ans: Erosion) and definition-based short notes on agents of erosion.
Keywords to drop in an answer

degradational / exogenetic force · dynamic · ex-situ · transporting agents · hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, corrosion · regolith removal · landform sculpting.
